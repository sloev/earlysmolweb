## Early connectivity and domain context (not personal-page evidence)

### Primary IANA .mh delegation (16 August 1996)
The country-code top-level domain `.mh` was created/delegated on 16 August 1996 according to the IANA root-whois and delegation records. Sponsoring organization was the Cabinet Office, Republic of the Marshall Islands. Administrative contact was listed as Amata Kabua (Cabinet Office). Technical contact was Steve R. Kaczkowski of Internet Connect Inc., Wauwatosa, Wisconsin, United States. Nameservers were US-based (ins2.tosa.twtelecom.net and ns1.tosa.twtelecom.net). Registration services URL was given as http://www.nic.net.mh/. This establishes the formal domain infrastructure date; it does not by itself prove local operational IP connectivity, local hosting, or public web publishing inside the Marshall Islands.

- Sources: IANA root-whois / delegation records (e.g. archived https://web.archive.org/web/20081203152847/http://www.iana.org/root-whois/mh.htm; contemporary compilations confirming Record created 16-August-1996). Current IANA DB: https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/.mh.html.
- Evidence grade: **A** (primary IANA record for delegation date and contacts).

### Commercial ISP service via ITE Guam / IT&E (from October 1996)
Contemporary reports establish that IT&E Overseas Inc. (ITE Guam, www.ite.com) was the sole ISP providing internet services to the Marshall Islands beginning in October 1996. A 2002 UNESCO survey of Pacific Islands internet infrastructure states: “IT&E Overseas Inc (www.ite.com) is the only ISP in the Marshall Islands. It first offered internet services in October of 1996.” A 12 December 2002 RNZ report confirms: “ITE Guam has been Marshall Islands’ provider since it went on-line in 1996.” NTA continued to rely on the Guam-based provider into 2003 while planning its own local server.

This is connectivity evidence (external-hosted ISP service), not local hosting or resident personal web publishing. User numbers remained low (UN estimates ~0.04 % in 1996).

- Sources:
  - UNESCO, *Internet Infrastructure and e-Governance in Pacific Islands Countries* (2002), Marshall Islands section: https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000149788
  - RNZ, “Marshall Islands internet users want more reliable service,” 12 December 2002: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/pacific/142111/marshall-islands-internet-users-want-more-reliable-service
  - RNZ, “Marshall Islands plans to set up internet service,” 20 May 2003: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/pacific/143667/marshall-islands-plans-to-set-up-internet-service
- Evidence grade: **B** (contemporary institutional survey and press reports).

### Late-1990s / early-2000s ISP notes
Secondary sources state that an ISP associated with the “MH” designation was founded around 1996–1997, but also report that the domain was largely inactive for local website registration for a period thereafter. A 2003 RNZ report indicates that the Marshall Islands National Telecommunications Authority (NTA) was still planning to become its own ISP (having previously used ITE in Guam), with equipment ordered for a server expected online by mid-July 2003, and was seeking government assistance to regain control of the `.mh` domain for local email and websites. APNIC ASN 24439 for NTA was allocated later (2005).

- Sources:
  - Wikipedia “Communications in the Marshall Islands” (archived versions noting ISP “MH” founded 1996–1997 and subsequent inactivity of local website registrations).
  - RNZ, “Marshall Islands plans to set up internet service,” 20 May 2003: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/pacific/143667/marshall-islands-plans-to-set-up-internet-service
  - APNIC whois for AS24439 (NTA).
- Evidence grade: **B/C** (2003 contemporary press for NTA plan; secondary for 1996–97 ISP foundation).

### Infrastructure notes
Telephone and satellite infrastructure existed in the mid-1990s (Majuro, Ebeye, Kwajalein). High-speed undersea-cable connectivity (HANTRU-1) arrived much later (2010). No contemporary 1995–1998 personal-homepage or public web-publishing evidence has been located in readily available sources. Estimated internet users appear from 1996 in later UN compilations (very low initial figures).

## January 1997 RMI Online directory lead

Mark C. Goniwiecha's “Guam & Micronesia in Cyberspace,” *Journal of the Pacific Society*, January 1997, lists **“RMI Online, Republic of the Marshall Islands”** at:

http://www.clark.net/pub/rmiemb

This is a contemporary directory reference for a Marshall Islands-labelled page by January 1997. The host is clark.net, not a local .mh domain. The path “rmiemb” may suggest an embassy-related page, but the original page was not recovered, so its operator, official status, content and purpose remain unknown. The directory does not identify an author or establish local hosting, resident authorship, or personal expression. No Wayback capture was recovered through this access route.
