# Greenland — early personal-web research

**Status:** infrastructure leads found; no personal-site candidate verified yet. Atagu (atagu.gl) remains a probable 1995 institutional web-news publication; earliest public archive capture is 1999.
**Priority:** first wave.
**Research jurisdiction:** Greenland (Kalaallit Nunaat), within the Kingdom of Denmark.

## Context leads (not personal-page claims)

- **1995-08-01:** A 1997 Nordic library technology report says Greennet, the Greenland part of the Internet, was commissioned as a pilot project on 1 August 1995; public nationwide access through TELE Greenland began 1 January 1996. It identifies early Greenland information providers including Pilersuiffik/Inerisaavik, KANUKOKA, TELE Greenland, and Home Rule authorities. This is useful for reconstructing the first local hosting and institutional network, but it does not identify a personal homepage.
  - Source: [State-of-the-art of information technologies in libraries in the Nordic countries: 1997](https://librarytechnology.org/docs/21695.pdf)
- **1995-09-01:** An NSRC connectivity archive preserves a contemporaneous announcement by Q-Data's Ivan Andersen describing a Greenland-connected network and internet browsing/email access, with a link spanning Qaanaaq to Nanortalik. The post calls it probably the world's northernmost Internet site; treat this as an ISP/company announcement, not a personal site.
  - Source: [NSRC — Internet goes north - to Greenland](https://www.nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=GL&id=890202496237%3A497432310)
- NSRC's Greenland index lists further contemporary leads from February and January 1995 ("Greenland Internet"; "Gossip GL - Greenland with Full Internet Link"), plus Q-Data, TELE Greenland, Pilersuiffik, and ATAGU. These may expose link directories, early users, or local technical correspondents.
  - Source: [NSRC — Greenland connectivity archive index](https://www.nsrc.org/db/lookup/ISO%3DGL)
- A 2017 scholarly chapter gives a December 1995 date for a first Greenlandic web news site but names it `sermitsiaq.gl` while also attributing it to Atassut. Other sources identify the first Internet newspaper as **Atagu**, operated by Atassut; treat the 2017 name as a conflicting/possibly mistaken attribution rather than silently repeating it. See the evidence note below.

## Newly inspected contemporary NSRC reports — January–February 1995

Two previously listed NSRC records have now been inspected directly:

- **23 January 1995 — “Gossip GL - Greenland with Full Internet Link”** lists three hosts: `gw.srpo.gl`, `saarullik.srpo.gl`, and `umimmak.srpo.gl`. The report includes a traceroute and ping to `umimmak.srpo.gl` from the UK, demonstrating that the host was externally reachable. This is network infrastructure evidence only; no personal page is identified. https://www.nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=GL&id=890202496466:497429432
- **14 February 1995 — “Greenland Internet”** explains that the class C network `192.101.148.0` was established around 1990 for the Sondrestrom radar facility in Kangerlussuaq, operated by SRI International for NSF and connected to NASA Science Internet by satellite via NASA Goddard. The report is a technical correspondence from Yadunath Zambre at SRI, not evidence of a locally authored personal site. https://www.nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=GL&id=890202496341:497430927

These records refine the chronology: Greenland had externally reachable research-network hosts by January 1995, but the network was tied to the Sondrestrom radar and NASA/NSF research infrastructure. They do not prove ordinary Greenland residents had web hosting or that a personal homepage existed. Follow-up should inspect later September 1995 Q-Data/TELE Greenland records and the early hostnames for user directories, while distinguishing locally authored pages from NASA/SRI technical infrastructure.


## Contemporary Q-Data announcement — 1 September 1995

A contemporaneous message from Ivan Andersen of Q-Data, preserved in the NSRC Connectivity Providers Database, says the Greenland IT provider connected an internal network spanning Qaanaaq (Thule) to Nanortalik to the Internet using links supplied by Tele Greenland and UNI-C. The announcement explicitly lists email and browsing access. It is strong evidence for an operational Greenland-connected provider network and user-facing email/Web access by 1 September 1995, rather than merely a planned project.

- Operator: Q-Data, Greenland IT service provider; named correspondent Ivan Andersen.
- Infrastructure partners: Tele Greenland and UNI-C (Danish provider).
- Service: internal network connectivity, email and Web browsing; the announcement does not identify the first public website, individual homepage, or exact start date.
- Status: **verified for the provider's own announcement and described operational service on 1 September 1995**; independent corroboration and original service logs remain desirable.
- Source: https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=GL&id=890202496237:497432310.

A follow-up technical exchange the same day disputes the claim that this was the world's northernmost Internet site, identifying the University Centre in Svalbard as already connected. This is useful evidence of external scrutiny and confirms that the Q-Data announcement should not be repeated as an uncontested global superlative. Source: https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=GL&id=890202496109:497433692.

### Interpretation

The 1995 Q-Data service is a Greenland-based commercial/institutional network milestone. It must not be conflated with the earlier Sondrestrom/NASA research network, nor treated as evidence of an individual's personal expression. The 1995–96 public-access chronology in the Nordic library report remains a separate nationwide-access milestone.

## Corrected access chronology — Greenet, Q-Data and commercial service

The earlier summary compressed several different rollout stages. TELE Greenland's own bilingual corporate history now provides a more precise sequence:

- **1 July 1995 — Greenland's international Internet link put into use:** a 64-kbps fixed satellite connection via UNI-C in Copenhagen to DENet. TELE Greenland named the connection **Greenet**.
- **August 1995 — selected trial users connected:** the corporate history explicitly describes these as test users, not a nationwide retail launch.
- **1 January 1996 — commercial Internet subscriptions opened:** customers paid a connection fee and fixed quarterly subscription. By year-end the company reports **528 modem subscribers and six institutions on dedicated circuits**.

Source: TELE Greenland/Tusass, *Tele- og posthistorien*, pp. 92–95, 1995–96 timeline: https://images.tusass.gl/organization/history/magazine.pdf (especially PDF text pp. 1591–1640).

This reconciles the 1997 Nordic library technology report's August 1995 pilot / January 1996 public-access wording: July marks the international link entering service, August the selected trial-user phase, and January 1996 commercial retail availability. Do not use these dates interchangeably.

### Q-Data's parallel provider network — September 1995

The 1 September 1995 Q-Data announcement says its internal network, extending from Qaanaaq (Thule) to Nanortalik, was connected to the Internet through Tele Greenland and UNI-C, with email and Web browsing. A 7 September 1995 external traceroute to `kaasassuk.qdata.gl` independently reached the Q-Data host; the NSRC correspondent described Greenland as having a commercial provider for full Internet access.

- Operator: Q-Data, a Greenland IT service provider; the announcement was sent by Ivan Andersen.
- Service: Internet-connected internal network with email and Web browsing; the exact customer classes and launch date are not specified.
- Sources: NSRC, “Internet goes north - to Greenland” (1 Sep 1995): https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=GL&id=890202496237:497432310 ; NSRC, “Ping from GL” (7 Sep 1995): https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=GL&id=890202495978:497435143.
- Status: **verified for the announced service and external reachability by September 1995**. Keep this distinct from Greenet's national backbone and TELE Greenland's January 1996 retail launch.

## Early web publication — Atagu, December 1995 (not a personal homepage)

Several sources identify **Atagu** as Greenland's first Internet newspaper:

- A chapter by Jette Rygaard says the first web-based news site, Atagu, began in **December 1995** and continued until 2002; it attributes operation to the political party **Atassut**. Source: *Searching for Greenland's Youth Culture Before the Internet Age*, in *Lost Histories of Youth Culture* (2015), pp. 75–76: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324858580_The_Nikkeijin_Underground_in_Japanese_Extreme_Metal (full-text chapter).
- A legal article originally published in *Ugeskrift for Retsvæsen* **1996B.263** describes Atagu as a new Internet newspaper that had already ceased publication. It does **not** name the editor. The article is reproduced with permission by the Greenlandic legal-profession association: https://dgl.gl/pages/artikler/artikler_tekst.php?id=9.
- A 1999 Nordicom research volume also calls Atagu the first Internet newspaper started by Atassut: https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1534713/FULLTEXT01.pdf.
- Later academic work cites the site as `www.atagu.gl`; this supports a likely original domain, but no 1995 WARC/Wayback capture or contemporaneous launch notice has yet been recovered: https://www.uni.gl/media/40792/steenrjeppsonspeciale.pdf.
- An additional 2015 chapter gives the same December 1995 date but names the site `sermitsiaq.gl` while attributing it to Atassut. That conflicts with the multiple Atagu identifications above and should be treated as a likely naming error until the underlying source is checked: https://www.uni.gl/media/4957793/mediespejlet.pdf.

**Assessment:** Atagu is a **probable** early Greenlandic web-news publication, with a 1995 start supported retrospectively and its existence and cessation by 1996 documented in a contemporary legal article. The 1996 article does not identify its editor; the later link to Anders Nilsson and Kamikposten comes from retrospective sources. It is an online newspaper/political publication, not a personal homepage. Exact launch day, original capture, and precise URL history remain open. Do not assert that no earlier Greenlandic website existed.

## Atagu identification strengthened; conflicting domain attribution narrowed (2018–2026)

Two further Greenland media references now support the **Atagu / atagu.gl** identification and operator chronology:

- The 2018 report *Tusagassiuutit 2018: en kortlægning af de grønlandske medier*, produced by Ilisimatusarfik and hosted by Roskilde University, states that **Kamikposten (formerly atagu.gl) had existed since 1995** and was run by former Atassut politician **Anders Nilsson**. This gives a retrospective continuity account from the early title/domain to a later successor and a named operator; it does not provide an original 1995 capture. See printed report pp. 12–13 / PDF lines 374–386: https://rucforsk.ruc.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/63776085/dktusagassiuutit2018rapport.pdf.
- The current Lex.dk national encyclopedia entry “Grønlandske medier”, authored by Martine Lind Krebs and last changed 21 September 2026, states that Kamikposten began as Greenland's first internet newspaper in **1995** under the name **atagu.gl**, operated by the Atassut party. It distinguishes the later, unrelated kamikposten.gl site. https://lex.dk/gr%C3%B8nlandske_medier (see lines 73–81).

These are retrospective sources, not a surviving 1995 capture or contemporary launch notice. They materially strengthen the attribution of the 1995 internet newspaper to **Atagu / atagu.gl** and Atassut, and identify Anders Nilsson as the later operator/editor of Kamikposten. The contemporary 1996 legal article says Atagu had already ceased publication, so do not imply uninterrupted operation into the later Kamikposten era. No launch day is established.

**Conflict update:** the 2017 chapter's isolated wording that the December 1995 site was `sermitsiaq.gl` while operated by Atassut is now in tension with the 2018 research report and 2026 Lex entry, both of which identify Atagu/atagu.gl and its continuity into Kamikposten. Treat the `sermitsiaq.gl` string as a probable naming/attribution error unless a separate contemporary capture or primary source establishes otherwise; do not silently rewrite it as fact.

**Next evidence target:** recover original Atagu pages, domain registration/DNS records, party publications or contemporaneous newspaper notices from 1995–96. Search for the earliest Wayback/Netarkivet capture of `atagu.gl` and any preserved server/FTP directory.

## Wayback Machine calendar check — atagu.gl (2026-11-16)

Direct inspection of the Internet Archive Wayback Machine calendar for `atagu.gl` shows:

- **119 captures** between **21 April 1999** and 11 March 2023.
- **No captures** in 1995, 1996, 1997 or 1998.

This is negative evidence only: the absence of early public crawls does not disprove a 1995 launch, but it confirms that no surviving public archive snapshot from the claimed launch year is currently available via the Wayback Machine. Any verification of a December 1995 start must therefore come from other sources (Netarkivet if it holds .gl material, party archives, contemporary press, or private server backups).

Source: https://web.archive.org/web/*/atagu.gl (calendar view inspected 2026-11-16).
