# Timor-Leste — early internet activity

**Status:** scouting (ccTLD and post-1999 UN/UNDP connectivity documented; pre-independence diaspora and activist online activity noted; no local personal homepage verified yet)

## Internet context

- **.tp ccTLD (predecessor):** Delegated 9 May 1997 for Portuguese Timor (grade A for DNS registration only; IANA). Operated from Ireland by Connect-Ireland in cooperation with East Timorese authorities in exile; first symbolic site freedom.tp registered around December 1997. Does not prove local hosting or resident operators in the territory under occupation. Retired in favour of .tl in 2015 after independence and migration.
- **.tl ccTLD:** Delegated 23 March 2005 to Autoridade Nacional de Comunicações / Department of Information Technology (grade A for DNS only). Second-level registration open worldwide; no local-presence requirement.
- **Post-1999 connectivity (after destruction of infrastructure):** First documented Internet connection in the territory associated with UN/UNDP support for UNTAET. UNDP Internet Network for Development Project first connection dated 2 February 2000 (64 kbps, upgraded to 128 kbps in April; later higher), initially serving UN agencies in the same building; later extended to government, NGOs, diplomatic missions (contemporary NSRC-archived report by Michael Mwaura, 13 August 2002, grade B). Three separate gateways 2000–mid-2002: UN system, UNDP project, and Telstra (only the latter commercial, per 2006 government account). Commercial gateway later under Timor Telecom BOT contract from July 2002.
- **Pre-independence activist / diaspora activity (1990s):** Online campaigning via email lists, newsgroups (e.g. reg.easttimor), and websites hosted outside the territory (Australia, Ireland, etc.). Minihub ISP (Australia-based) provided clandestine email and web hosting for independence-struggle participants (retrospective grade C). First computers in the territory reported early 1990s (grade C).
