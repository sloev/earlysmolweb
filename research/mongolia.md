## Internet context

### Datacom / MagicNet and the first email service

The strongest early local evidence is the contemporary NSRC correspondence preserved in its Connectivity Providers Database.

- **14 October 1994 — service preparation:** D. Enkhbat told E-LAW that Datacom's Waffle system was ready to begin service on Monday 17 October, with a 38.4 Kbps ZyXEL modem and a registered UUCP account. This documents preparation of the local system, not yet a successful international exchange. [NSRC, “Mongolia, e-mail”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=922164469522:489026752).
- **28 October 1994 — UUCP configuration:** Steve Fram sent replacement UUCP software for Waffle's uucico/uux components and instructions for larger packet sizes. [NSRC, “files for UUCP connection”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=922164788952:488944071).
- **30 November 1994 — experimental exchange:** Datacom's system operator reported that the experimental connection had succeeded, thanking IGC for its assistance. [NSRC, “Mongolia's first email connection”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=922165327195:488917483).
- **8 December 1994 — UUCP feed:** an NSRC update described a Waffle-based UUCP feed from IGC, with the rate structure and polling schedule still being arranged. [NSRC, “Mongolia update (8 Dec. '94)”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=890202385461:497429855).
- **15 December 1994 — local BBS described:** Datacom reported a 16-line online BBS with teleconference, email, file library, databases, text search/retrieval, shopping-mall and QWK-mail functions. The basic package included unlimited domestic email and limited international email. Datacom asked IGC to call its node every day except weekends and said it was still inexperienced with international email and Usenet exchange. This distinguishes the local BBS from its scheduled international gateway. [NSRC, “Information needed”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=890202385338:497431138).
- **27 March 1995 — service claim by operator:** Enkhbat stated that Internet email service had operated since December 1994, that IGC now called once daily, and that the 16-line BBS was available to Mongolians and foreigners who requested access. The same message says a full Internet connection was still being planned. [NSRC, “(MN) Mongolia update (3.95)”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=890202385196:497432580).
- **23 August 1995 — expanded network:** Datacom reported more than 450 subscribers, an 8-line PC-Mail/dial-up email server, a separate 16-line Magic BBS (up to 14.2 Kbps), and a Waffle DOS gateway for Internet email and news. An X.25 network to the provinces was in progress; full Internet access remained a goal, not yet an accomplished service. [NSRC, “MN - MagicNet Update (8/95)”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=890202385046:497433999).

**Date qualification:** Datacom's own 1994–95 reports put the experimental connection on 30 November 1994 and the beginning of Internet email service in December 1994. Oliver Corff's 1995 Mongolia FAQ instead dates the first email link to January/February 1995 and describes it as non-continuous. These may refer to experiment, service start and a demonstrably working user link at different stages; the available evidence does not justify collapsing them into one exact launch day. [FAQ, posted 20 March 1995](https://groups.google.com/g/soc.culture.mongolian/c/HzGxuFOY71k).

### Full-time Internet and web publishing

- **2 March 1995 — .mn domain record created:** the nameserver/registration information reproduced in the 5 January 1996 NSRC announcement gives a creation date of 2 March 1995 and identifies Datacom's Dangaasuren Enkhbat in Ulaanbaatar as administrative contact. This is DNS administration, not evidence of a website or resident personal publishing. [NSRC, “Mongolia is on the Internet”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=890202384717:497424020).
- **5 January 1996 — routed link verified:** Steve Goldstein relayed Sprint's confirmation that the 128 Kbps satellite link to Datacom's router in Ulaanbaatar was responding to pings. The report verifies reachability to the router, not that every service was already available to every customer. [NSRC, “Mongolia is on the Internet”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=890202384717:497424020).
- **9 January 1996 — Internet through the BBS:** Enkhbat described the 128 Kbps satellite link, two Sun Netra servers, Cisco router and 16-line BBS. He said BBS users could access the Internet through Telnet/FTP and that the BBS supported a web service for Internet call-in; public commercial service was still planned, not yet started. [NSRC, “Mongolia on the Net (1/96)”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=890202384567:497425725).
- **17 January 1996 — public opening ceremony:** Datacom reported that more than 100 people from 60 organisations attended the Internet centre's opening. The report was sent on 18 January. [NSRC, “Opening ceremony [Internet in Mongolia]”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=MN&id=890202401911:497433357).
- **January 1996 — MongoliaOnline:** the .mn registry's retrospective company timeline says the first version of [www.mol.mn](http://www.mol.mn) went online in January 1996. It describes a commercial portal, not a personal homepage. The original page and an independent contemporary capture have not yet been recovered. [.mn registry / Datacom history](https://www.cctld.mn/about-us).

## Personal web ecology and cross-border community

- **No verified resident personal homepage yet.** Searches in English and Mongolian Cyrillic have not produced a dated personal site with a defensible author-residence link to Mongolia. This is a search gap, not evidence that none existed.
- **Usenet community lead:** Oliver Corff's 1995 FAQ says the unmoderated soc.culture.mongolian newsgroup began in spring 1994 and carried discussions and current-event contributions. The FAQ itself was authored from a Free University Berlin account; the location of individual contributors is not established. This is a Mongolia-focused language/community connection, not proof of a Mongolia-hosted service or resident authorship. [Google Groups, FAQ posted 20 March 1995](https://groups.google.com/g/soc.culture.mongolian/c/HzGxuFOY71k).
- **Transnational Gopher/Web material:** the same FAQ identifies a Gopher service called “Infosystem Mongolei” at Free University Berlin, operating from November 1993, and a later WWW interface at userpage.fu-berlin.de/~corff/. The operator/hosting basis is Berlin, Germany; this is relevant to Mongolian studies and diaspora/community research, not a local Mongolian server. [FAQ, 15 November 1996](https://groups.google.com/g/soc.culture.mongolian/c/AzEbVg5yk-k).
- **Mongolia Society homepage:** the 1996 FAQ says the Mongolia Society in Bloomington, Indiana, established a WWW homepage in summer 1995, authored by Mitch Rice. This is an overseas institutional/community site; the FAQ does not establish that Rice was resident in Mongolia. It also points to his Mongolia WWW Virtual Library and personal “Mongolia Homepage.” [FAQ, 21 March 1996](https://groups.google.com/g/soc.culture.mongolian/c/GGs1TESRN5g).
- **MagicNet web publishing lead:** the November 1996 FAQ says MagicNet's www.magic.mn offered Mongolia news and daily downloads of Today / Önöödör articles, with a downloadable font. This is a commercial local-provider publishing lead; exact launch date and original capture remain unverified. [FAQ, 15 November 1996](https://groups.google.com/g/soc.culture.mongolian/c/AzEbVg5yk-k).
- **Ger e-magazine lead:** a later UNDP project retrospective describes Ger as Mongolia's first e-magazine, with four issues between 1998 and 2000, including a launch issue about youth and social transition. It was a collective publication involving Mongolian and foreign journalists, not a personal site. Original issue URLs/captures and exact publication metadata still need checking. [David South Consulting, UN/UNDP Mongolia case study](https://dsconsulting.squarespace.com/case-studies/case-study-4-un-undp-mongolia-1997-1999.html).
- **UNDP Mongolia portal lead:** the same retrospective says the bilingual UNDP Mongolia portal launched in 1997 and became one of the country's first large-scale portals. Institutional, not personal; original captures need recovery.

## Earliest known examples by category

### Connectivity and infrastructure

- **Verified:** Datacom's local service preparation and experimental UUCP exchange in October–November 1994; first Internet email service reported by Datacom as December 1994. The exact first operational date remains disputed by the 1995 FAQ's January/February 1995 wording.
- **Verified:** .mn domain record created 2 March 1995 (DNS milestone only).
- **Verified:** full-time 128 Kbps satellite/IP link to Datacom's router working by 5 January 1996; public Internet centre opened 17 January 1996.

### Communication and community

- **Verified local service:** Datacom's 16-line BBS and Waffle/UUCP email gateway, documented December 1994 and expanded by 1995. The BBS itself was a local dial-up service; international Internet email/news was a scheduled gateway, not a permanent IP connection.
- **Candidate country-community connection:** soc.culture.mongolian, reported as active from spring 1994. Its creation and contributors' locations need independent verification; do not count it as local resident activity yet.

### Web publishing

- **Probable earliest identified local portal:** www.mol.mn / MongoliaOnline, first version dated January 1996 by the .mn registry's retrospective timeline. Commercial portal; no original page capture inspected.
- **Lead:** www.magic.mn, provider news and newspaper-download service mentioned in 1996.
- **Lead:** UNDP Mongolia portal, reported launched 1997.
- **Candidate:** Ger, collective online magazine, 1998–2000.
- **No verified personal homepage** with an established resident author and dated original or archive capture.

## Evidence and chronology notes

- The 1994 Datacom material is unusually strong because the NSRC archive preserves dated operator-to-operator messages, not merely later anniversary claims.
- The local BBS's 16 telephone lines were not 16 international Internet connections. International email used scheduled UUCP/Waffle transfers to IGC; later reports describe a separate full-time satellite/IP link.
- The date discrepancy between Datacom's December 1994 service statement and the FAQ's January/February 1995 link date is retained. The experimental exchange of 30 November, operator-reported service start, and later public confirmation may be different milestones.
- The January 1996 NSF/Sprint report confirms reachability to the router. It does not prove every local service or every customer's access was fully operational at that instant.
- The 1996 MongoliaOnline date is retrospective and needs an original capture or contemporary launch notice before being treated as a verified first website.
- No local archive/Wayback capture of mol.mn, magic.mn or Ger was inspected in this pass. Direct Wayback CDX/replay requests were inaccessible through the available web interface; this is a tooling limitation, not a null archive result.

## 2026-11-17 — Early web publishing and personal-page leads

### MN-06 — S. Badral's unidentified Yahoo-associated Mongolia page

- **Status:** lead.
- **Creator:** S. Badral, likely Sanligiin Badral; the identity is supported by later Mongolian-language technology profiles, but the original page has not been recovered.
- **Activity/type:** an individually created website about Mongolia, described by a first-person witness as “Yahoo дахь монголын тухай вэб” (“a website about Mongolia on Yahoo”). This is not enough to call it a personal homepage, and “on Yahoo” must not be silently converted into Yahoo GeoCities or a hosting claim.
- **Date:** reported as already known at the Mongolia Internet opening event in Ulaanbaatar on 17 January 1996. Exact creation date is unknown.
- **Country connection:** creator is identified as a Mongolian technology contributor and was met at the local opening event; residence at publication and actual hosting location are not established.
- **Evidence:** Jagdagdorj Erkhembayar's first-person retrospective in the Mongolian Ministry of Digital Development's 2023 volume, chapter 6.7, pp. 455–456. The memoir was written for the Internet's 15th anniversary (2011) and recalls meeting Badral at the 17 January 1996 opening. It calls this page almost the only website/online information about Mongolia at the time. Grade B: named first-person recollection, written retrospectively.
- **Unresolved:** original URL, whether Yahoo was host, directory or discovery platform, earliest dated capture, and Badral's residence when the page was published.

### MN-07 — Sanligiin Badral, “Mongolian Language Tools” (badaa.mngl.net)

- **Status:** candidate for an individual-created topical website; not yet established as a personal homepage.
