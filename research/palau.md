# Palau — early Internet activity, publishing and online communities

**Status:** scouting; initial connectivity leads plus January 1997 directory-listed institutional and collective pages documented; no personal homepage or online community is verified.  
**Scope:** Palau is covered as one of the 195 baseline countries. The .pw ccTLD is recorded as infrastructure only; it is not used to infer any person's nationality.\n**Last checked:** 2026-11-22

## Internet context

The earliest located public-access lead is the Palau National Communications Corporation's (PNCC) PalauNet dial-up service. A PNCC/Asia-Pacific Telecommunity report says it launched on 17 March 1997 and introduced international Internet and e-mail access to Palau. That exact date is retrospective, not a surviving 1997 announcement. Contemporary NSRC records establish that PNCC/PalauNet was operating by January 2000: PNCC reported 1,000 Internet accounts, 33.6-kbps dial-up, ISDN and HDSL access, and a 192-kbps international connection through Sprint.

IANA's 2003 report says the .pw country-code top-level domain was established in May 1997. This is a separate DNS-administration milestone; it does not demonstrate that a website, mail list, or personal page was already operating under .pw.

The same PNCC history dates two additional developments to 2001: LocalNet, a low-cost service for accessing websites within Palau, and audio streaming of local radio station WWFM to the World Wide Web. These are useful early local-web and online-broadcast leads, but both dates are retrospective and neither has yet been matched to a contemporary announcement or surviving capture.

## Earliest located examples by category

### Connectivity

- **17 March 1997 — PalauNet dial-up launch (candidate).** PNCC's 2013 history gives this exact launch date and says the service brought international Internet access to Palau. A 2017 local news report independently repeats the launch year, quoting a PNCC press release. January 2000 NSRC reports provide strong contemporary confirmation that the service was operational, but do not independently establish the 1997 start date.
- **By 31 January 2000 — operational public Internet service.** PNCC's technical response to NSRC reported 1,000 Internet accounts, 48 dial-up ports at 33.6 kbps, 16 ISDN ports, 32 HDSL ports, and 192 kbps of international bandwidth through Sprint. This is the strongest contemporary connectivity evidence found in this pass.

### E-mail and communication

- **1997 — international e-mail access via PalauNet (candidate).** The 2013 PNCC history places e-mail access alongside the March 1997 dial-up launch. A January 2000 PNCC technical message used a palaunet.com e-mail address, and a 2017 PNCC notice said existing PalauNet e-mail addresses would be retained when dial-up ended. No original 1997 message, mailing-list archive, or named online community has been located.

### DNS and infrastructure

- **May 1997 — .pw ccTLD established (verified as a delegation milestone).** IANA's 30 June 2003 redelegation report states that .pw was established in May 1997. This records the namespace delegation, not a first website or the nationality of any registrant.

### Web publishing and local services

- **By 25 January 2000 — PNCC/PalauNet website referenced (candidate).** A contemporary NSRC report points readers to www.palaunet.com and records a traceroute reaching pnccwg.palaunet.com through Sprint. The NSRC provider record, created 28 January 2000, links to http://www.palaunet.com/. This establishes a contemporary reference to the provider's website, not its first publication date or the first Palauan website.
- **2001 — LocalNet (candidate).** PNCC's retrospective history describes a low-cost service for websites within Palau, with local access charges waived and international e-mail access effectively available without time charges. The specific sites and their authors remain unidentified.
- **2001 — WWFM audio stream on the Web (candidate).** PNCC's retrospective history says it introduced audio streaming by putting local radio station WWFM on the World Wide Web. No stream URL, contemporary announcement, or archived capture has yet been found.

## January 1997 Pacific Society directory leads

Mark C. Goniwiecha's “Guam & Micronesia in Cyberspace,” *Journal of the Pacific Society*, January 1997, lists two Palau-associated pages under “Internet Resources and Home Pages”:

- **FullMoon Online Magazine of Palau** — http://virtual.guam.net/fullmoon/index.html. The directory title suggests a collective Palau-focused online publication. Its hostname is under virtual.guam.net, so the page was externally hosted; author residence, editorial operator, and physical hosting location are unknown.
- **Palau Visitors Authority, Republic of Palau** — http://www.visit-palau.com. The directory describes the page as the Palau Visitors Authority, but the page itself was not recovered, so the operator's official status and content remain unverified.

Both are dated directory references by January 1997, not page launch dates. The article lists personal, institutional, commercial and community resources together; these entries are not personal-homepage evidence. No original content or Wayback capture was recovered through this access route.
