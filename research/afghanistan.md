# Afghanistan — early internet activity

**Status:** scouting  
**ISO:** AF

## Internet context

Afghanistan’s early internet history is shaped by decades of conflict. Contemporary accounts indicate that the country was almost completely offline in the 1990s. Under the Taliban (late 1990s–2001) non-governmental use of email and websites was prohibited; reports state that violations could be punishable by death and that the sole permitted computer with internet access was at Taliban headquarters (access via Pakistan). After the fall of the Taliban in late 2001, public and commercial internet services began to appear in 2002–2003.

- **.af ccTLD:** First delegated by IANA in October 1997 to Abdul Razeeq (administrative contact, described as of Kabul) with Hostmaster / NetNames of London as technical contact. NetNames provided temporary free registration and DNS until a stable local registry could be established. Due to the civil war, new registrations were later halted while DNS resolution continued. Redelegated in 2002–2003 to the Ministry of Communications (with UNDP technical support). Public activation of the domain announced March 2003; first two sites were Ministry of Communications (moc.gov.af) and UNDP (undp.org.af).  
  **Evidence grade:** A (IANA redelegation report 2003; contemporary press). DNS delegation ≠ local hosting or resident authorship.

- **Public access milestones (post-2001):**  
  - July 2002: first reported internet café in Kabul (Intercontinental Hotel, 11 PCs, 128 kbps satellite).  
  - July 2003: UNDP / Ministry of Communications telekiosks in Kabul post offices offering low-cost public access (contemporary Wired / UNDP reporting).  
  **Evidence grade:** B (contemporary press).

No verified locally hosted personal homepage or resident-authored web publication from the 1990s has been located in current sources. Pre-2000 online activity connected to Afghanistan is primarily diaspora-based (external hosting).
