# Denmark — early personal-web research

**Status:** early personal-page candidates identified; earliest claim not independently verified from primary captures. Strong contemporary evidence for named personal homepage presence by February 1995 (Michael Maardt).
**Scope:** Denmark as a country entry, separate from Greenland and the Faroe Islands. Pages created by residents in Denmark are distinguished from pages by Danish/Faroese authors who were resident abroad.

## Internet and web-archive context

- The Royal Danish Library's research project *History of the Danish web 1992–1997* studies the early Danish web using material acquired from the Internet Archive and preserved in Netarkivet as WARC files, searchable through SolrWayback. The project places the first Danish website in 1992, major growth in spring 1995, and the opening of .dk domain registration to private individuals in January 1997. This is a key route for systematic extraction of early personal sites.
  - Source: https://www.kb.dk/en/services/cultural-heritage-research-and-study/history-danish-web-1992-1997
  - Project timeline: https://webhistorie.dk/timeline-2/
  - Project site (Niels Brügger): http://webhistorie.dk/

## Pre-Web network-service chronology — DIKU, DKUUG and DEnet

The early Danish chronology predates personal homepages by more than a decade. It is important context for locating individual authors, but network services are not themselves personal publishing:

- **2 January 1983 — international email from DIKU:** the University of Copenhagen's Department of Computer Science (DIKU) documents that Keld Simonsen sent a test email from Denmark to a contact in the Netherlands and received a reply through the European network. The department's history says email was processed when systems dialled one another, roughly every four hours. By 1984 ten additional Danish computers had joined and access to email was offered to researchers for DKK 200 annually. Source: https://di.ku.dk/english/about/department_history/new_opportunities_in_research/.
- **1983 — EUnet/DKUUG connection and broader UUCP services:** Keld Simonsen's archival collection reproduces contemporary documents from 1982–84, including EUnet maps, correspondence, invoices and the 1987 .dk registration. This is a particularly valuable primary-source route for reconstructing the earliest network topology. Source: https://www.open-std.org/keld/internetstart/.
- **1980s — UUCP mail and Usenet:** a Danish retrospective by Lars P. Fischer says DKUUG initially provided Usenet to members using a UUCP feed between Amsterdam's mcvax and DKUUG; member companies could obtain UUCP email and news. It also records a DAIMI UUCP feed to DIKU and onward to IESD in Aalborg, and says UNI-C began supplying IP traffic on the first research network in 1987. Treat this as a participant recollection and use the surviving maps/logs to verify node-level dates. Source: https://www.usenet.dk/firserne.html.
- **1988–89 — DEnet and NORDUnet:** a 2015 Danish infrastructure history says Copenhagen University joined DEnet in 1988 through EUnet in the Netherlands, initially supporting email and Usenet discussion exchange; interactive terminal access followed in 1989 when DEnet connected to the first-generation NORDUnet. Source: https://deic.dk/sites/default/files/2025-09/DeiC_A%CC%8Arsrapport_2015.pdf.
- **1 February 1994 — commercial/academic Internet service catalogue:** a contemporary contact directory describes UNI-C's Internet Service as offering email, Gopher, Veronica, Archie, FTP, Telnet, Usenet, personal file areas and resource information, via modem or DEnet. This documents a broad service ecosystem, but is not evidence that every service was hosted in Denmark or that users had personal websites. Source: https://cds.cern.ch/record/2806515/files/Dimou-email-contacts-1994.pdf.

### Interpretation and research value

The strongest early milestone is the dated 2 January 1983 DIKU email, supported by the university's institutional history and a surviving documentary collection. The 1980s UUCP/Usenet evidence supplies concrete routes into named nodes and operators. Keep email, UUCP, Usenet, research-network IP and later Web hosting as separate categories. Next, inspect the 1982–87 primary documents and surviving UUCP maps for individual system operators, personal accounts and early home-directory services; continue the separate Madsen Smed homepage capture search.


## Contemporary personal-homepage evidence — Michael Maardt, 19 February 1995

The Royal Danish Library's DR archive catalogue provides unusually specific contemporary evidence for a Danish personal homepage before the previously noted November 1995 Madsen Smed candidate. The programme **Harddisken**, broadcast on **19 February 1995, 15:00–17:00 on DR P1**, included a segment titled “Nyt fra nettet: Udstil dit privatliv på din egen personlige homepage” (“Showcase your private life on your own personal homepage”) and explicitly says: “Meet Michael Maardt,” identified as the man behind the computer publication *Brug din PC optimalt*.

- **Person:** Michael Maardt.
- **Country connection:** Maardt's own biographical page says he was born in Copenhagen in 1952 and moved back to Denmark in 1977 after studying in Frankfurt and Bremen. His 1995 web presence is therefore associated with a Denmark-resident author, not merely a Danish-language or .dk-hosted page. Source: https://rent-postevand.dk/shop/Michael_Maardt.
- **Date:** 19 February 1995 is the broadcast date and establishes that the personal homepage was already being publicly discussed by then. It is **not** necessarily the page's creation date.
- **Contemporary evidence (grade B):** Royal Danish Library / DR archive catalogue record for *Harddisken*, event 19 February 1995: https://www.kb.dk/find-materiale/dr-arkivet/post/ds.radio:oai:io:5c9e7940-2f63-40d9-a916-eff4da0baf59. The catalogue describes the segment and names Maardt, but the recording/transcript and URL are not exposed in the accessible catalogue record.
- **Retrospective corroboration (grade C):** Maardt says he has made his website since 1995; his current history page states that `knowware.dk` was registered on **23 October 1995**: https://knowware.dk/om-hjemmesiden.shtml. His current site index labels KnowWare as a 1995 site: https://a33.dk/mmsites.shtml.
- **Important URL caveat:** `knowware.dk` cannot be assumed to be the address of the homepage discussed in February because Maardt dates that domain's registration to October 1995. The February page may have used another host/domain or a path on a provider's service; the original URL remains unknown.
- **Status:** **probable named Danish personal-homepage presence by 19 February 1995**. This is stronger and earlier contemporary evidence than the previously listed November 1995 Madsen Smed retrospective, but it does not by itself prove that Maardt's was Denmark's first personal homepage.

### Next verification steps

1. Obtain the DR recording or transcript for the 19 February 1995 *Harddisken* broadcast; the segment may state the URL or hosting service.
2. Search 1995 Danish Internet directories and contemporary press for “Michael Maardt”, “Brug din PC optimalt” and his homepage address.
3. Search Netarkivet/Internet Archive for the recovered URL and compare its earliest capture with the 23 October 1995 `knowware.dk` registration.
4. Keep the Madsen Smed November 1995 claim in the register: it may still be the earliest *family homepage* candidate, while Maardt is an earlier documented *individual personal homepage*.

A recent Danish teaching slide deck labels **Peder Madsen** “the father of Denmark's first private homepage” and gives the URL `http://smed.dalgashave.dk/peder/forside.html`. The deck supplies no launch date, contemporary attribution or supporting citation. This is a **low-confidence lead only**: it does not establish a 1995 page or a national-first claim. Preserve the exact historical URL and seek a dated Internet Archive/Netarkivet capture plus independent authorship and residence evidence.

### DR broadcast recovery route — recording metadata and collection status

The Royal Danish Library catalogue gives the exact transmission window for the Harddisken programme featuring Michael Maardt: **19 February 1995, 15:00–17:00, DR P1**. The catalogue description explicitly identifies the “Nyt fra nettet” segment as a feature about displaying one's private life on a personal homepage and names Maardt. This provides exact retrieval metadata for a targeted archive request, rather than a generic request for a 1995 broadcast.

The Library's Radio and TV Collection says DR broadcasts have been systematically collected from **1987 onward**, while a substantial part of the pre-2006 collection remains undigitised. Its DR-arkivet guidance explains that older material is being prepared and added progressively. Consequently, failure to find this 1995 episode in the current public online archive is not evidence that the recording is missing from the collection. Request the specific 19 February 1995 P1 recording (15:00–17:00) from the Radio and TV Collection and ask whether the “Nyt fra nettet” segment survives separately or within the full programme.

- Programme catalogue: https://www.kb.dk/find-materiale/dr-arkivet/post/ds.radio:oai:io:5c9e7940-2f63-40d9-a916-eff4da0baf59.
- Collection scope and digitisation status: https://www.kb.dk/find-materiale/samlinger/radio-og-tv-samlingen.
- DR-arkiv access and older-content status: https://www.kb.dk/find-materiale/soegeindgange/dr-arkivet.
- **Status:** contemporary broadcast metadata is strong; actual recording and homepage URL remain unrecovered. No national-first claim.


## Additional leads from webhistorie.dk timeline (2026-11-16)

Niels Brügger’s ongoing research project timeline (webhistorie.dk) supplies several named early Danish web milestones useful for personal-page scouting:

- **Helle Degn** is listed as the first Danish member of Parliament with a personal web page (mid-1995 context on the timeline).
- Uffe Ellemann Jensen (Venstre) launched a weekly newsletter (early political online presence).
- First Danish primary-school website: Stensgård Skole, Gladsaxe (1995).
- First amt (region) website: Aarhus Amt (November 1995).
- Spring 1995 is identified as the period of major growth of the Danish web.

These are institutional/political rather than purely personal-expression pages, but they supply concrete named authors, institutions and approximate dates that can be chased in Netarkivet/SolrWayback and contemporary directories. Helle Degn’s later site helledegn.dk is known from archive captures; the original mid-1990s URL and first capture remain open.

Source: http://webhistorie.dk/timeline-2/ (inspected 2026-11-16; last edit noted as 29 Sep 2026).
