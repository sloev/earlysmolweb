# Faroe Islands — early personal-web research

**Status:** connectivity and academic-network leads found; no personal-site candidate verified yet. Institutional web-publishing milestone (Sosialurin) documented for 1997.
**Priority:** first wave.
**Research jurisdiction:** Faroe Islands (Føroyar), within the Kingdom of Denmark.

## Context leads (not personal-page claims)

- **1995-01:** A contemporaneous report indexed by the Network Startup Resource Center says the University and a few research institutions in the campus area in Tórshavn were connected to the Internet in January 1995, via a 64 Kbit/s leased line to UNI-C in Lyngby, Denmark, over the CANTAT-3 cable.
  - Source: [NSRC — News on the fo-domain (31 July 1995)](https://www.nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=FO&id=890202445799%3A497435668)
- The report is a strong lead for identifying early Faroese network administrators, researchers, university accounts, and institutional host directories. It does not itself establish a personal homepage.

## Track A — next lead-finding actions

1. Search the early `.fo` domain and NSRC's other Faroe reports for hostnames, named researchers, and user accounts.
2. Search in Faroese and Danish: føroyskt internet, heimasíða, persónlig heimasíða, privat hjemmeside, internet Føroyar, vevstaður.
3. Follow personal pages linked from the University of the Faroe Islands and early research-institution pages; inspect link pages, guestbooks, and web rings.
4. Search contemporary Faroese newspapers, computer clubs, ISP announcements, and mailing lists for early web authors.

## Track B — evidence and write-up

- January 1995 is an institutional connectivity milestone, not a date for a personal website.
- Do not treat `.fo` hosting alone as proof that an author lived in the Faroe Islands.

## Individual personal-page lead — spring 1997 (attribution caveat)

### Elin Brimheim Heinesen's first personal homepage

In a retrospective Q&A published on 18 September 2008, Elin Brimheim Heinesen says she began her first homepage in **spring 1997** as an HTML-learning project and a personal online calling card/CV. She explicitly describes the motivation as publishing a personal expression of herself and says she had seen other personal homepages that fascinated her. This is a much stronger personal-expression lead than the institutional sites previously recorded.

- Author: Elin Brimheim Heinesen.
- Date claimed: spring 1997; retrospective author testimony, not yet corroborated by a dated capture.
- Original URL: unknown; her present sites list personal pages at https://heinesen.fo/ and https://heinesen.info/, but neither is established as the 1997 URL.
- Sources: https://heinesen.info/wp/blog/2008/09/18/at-vera-sjonligur-a-internetinum-er-tad-gott-ella-ringt/ (Q&A states first site began spring 1997); https://heinesen.fo/en/ (biographical page); https://fo.linkedin.com/in/elinheinesen (profile identifies Faroe Islands connection).
- **Attribution warning:** her own biography says she returned to Denmark in summer 1993 and lived/worked in Copenhagen for 25 years, only moving back to the Faroe Islands in 2007. Her 1997 homepage was therefore apparently created while she was resident in Denmark, not the Faroe Islands. Record as a Faroese-author/diaspora lead only; do not count as a geographically Faroese page unless stronger evidence establishes a local connection.
- Status: candidate for early personal publishing by a Faroese author; not a verified earliest Faroese-resident homepage.

The Faroese National Library's web archive collects .fo domains and selected Faroese sites on other domains, but it is not open access; researchers must apply for access. This offers a practical route to testing whether an early personal site survives. Source: https://landsbokasavnid.fo/fao-web-archive?_l=en

Next actions: identify the 1997 homepage URL through author contact or old link directories; request Faroese Web Archive access for known candidate domains; search 1996–2000 .fo directories for residents' own pages.


## Contemporary network evidence — January–July 1995

The NSRC archive provides a more precise service chronology than the later institutional summaries:

- **19 January 1995 — externally visible .fo routing:** a contemporaneous traceroute from Imperial College London reached `dk-gw.sleipnir.fo` (130.227.128.1) through the Danish/NORDUnet route. This demonstrates that a Faroese-labelled gateway hostname was visible and reachable from abroad by that date; it does not prove a locally operated public service or personal page. Source: https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=FO&id=890202446086:497432752.
- **25 January 1995 — connected, but no mail-capable machines yet:** Erik Lawaetz at UNI-C reported that the Faroe Islands were connected, but that his contacts had not yet brought up mail-capable machines. This is a useful warning against equating routing/domain visibility with a functioning local email service. Source: https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=FO&id=890202445976:497434210.
- **31 July 1995 — operational institutional mail and user access:** University of the Faroe Islands network administrator Jógvan Martin Grastein reported that the university and research institutions in Tórshavn had a 64 Kbit/s leased line to UNI-C via CANTAT-3; approximately 100 PCs, three Suns and one PC/SCO system were present. Campus and dial-in users used the university-maintained **SLEIPNIR** server for mail across the participating institutions. The named institutional domains included the university administration, Faroese-language, history/social science and natural science departments, museums, National Archives, National Library, food/environment institute and fisheries laboratory. A combined Web/FTP server was still being prepared and was expected within about a month; do not backdate that server to January. Source: https://www.nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=FO&id=890202445799:497435668.
- **29 February 1996 — planned private/commercial dial-up:** a contemporary Ariadne article says the Faroese Telephone Company would begin private Internet access on this date, estimating roughly 500 users. Source: https://www7.ariadne.ac.uk/issue/2/faroe/.

### Interpretation

This documents three distinct milestones: early routing visibility (January 1995), a working shared institutional mail service (by July 1995), and planned broader private/commercial access (February 1996). SLEIPNIR was a multi-institution mail server, not a personal website. The Web/FTP server mentioned in July was prospective, not yet verified as live.


## Institutional web publishing — Internet Sosialin / Sosialurin, 1997

Contemporary Faroese reporting documents the newspaper's move from selected trials to a searchable online edition. Keep the first online edition, the open trial service, and the later formal launch as separate milestones:

- **3 September 1997 — first edition online:** a 3 September 1998 anniversary article says the first edition of Sosialurin was placed on the Internet exactly one year earlier. It reports that the database then contained more than **7,400 articles and news briefs** from the preceding year. Source: https://in.fo/grein/internet-sosialurin-n-ggjum-hami.
- **29 September 1997 — open trial service described:** a contemporary article by Eirikur Lindenskov says anyone connected to the Internet could read Sosialurin free during the trial. The whole written paper was uploaded; readers could search the archive, receive email alerts when new material appeared, and read the paper from anywhere. The site was updated around 16:00 daily. Source: https://in.fo/grein/sosialurin-vordin-heimsumfatandi.
- The same 29 September article describes a searchable database, a modular Web interface, the newspaper's server at FarCom in Tórshavn, a planned mirror in Denmark, and reporters uploading their own material. It says the online workflow normally took staff about ten minutes a day. It also describes journalists working from Argir, Klaksvík, Toftir, Sandvík and Singapore, with copy moved over the Internet.
- **25 November 1997 — formal public launch:** a later anniversary account describes a launch reception at the newspaper's Argir offices, where culture minister Sámal Petur í Grund pressed the button to release Internet Sosialin publicly; it identifies edition no. 226 of 26 November as the first issue in that formal public-launch sequence. This should not replace the earlier 3 September first-online-edition date. Source: https://in.fo/news-detail/sosialurin-hevur-verid-toekur-a-netinum-i-20-ar.
- The publishing system was developed with Dynamic Paper / FarCom. A 3 September 1998 anniversary article explicitly credits Far-Com in Tórshavn with developing the system. The 1997 announcement describes the searchable database and modular interface.
- Earlier experiments with placing Sosialurin content online occurred in 1995 via Faroe News / Óli á Deild Olsen, followed by further trials in spring 1996.

**Assessment:** This is a verified institutional / commercial web-publishing story, not a personal homepage. The 1997 and 1998 contemporary reporting establishes an actual full-paper online edition, searchable archive, email alerts, remote editorial workflow and a named technical partner. The distinction between the first online edition (3 September) and the later formal public launch (25 November) must be preserved. Original 1997 page captures and the named staff at FarCom / Dynamic Paper remain useful follow-up targets.

The same Faroenet server also hosted early personal heimasíður and political-party pages by September 1997, providing a concrete lead for personal-page scouting.
