# Lao People's Democratic Republic

**Status:** scouting — experimental NPI/LaoNet email reported from December 1994; a separate 1994 Laonet/IDRC/STENO public-email-centre claim remains unresolved against the 1996 PAN-Laos chronology; .la delegated May 1996; first permanent full Internet link reported 1998 with public confirmation 2000; no verified resident personal homepage yet.

## Internet context

### Early email and store-and-forward

- **December 1994 — experimental NPI/LaoNet email:** ISOC's INET'99 paper reports a FidoNet-based email setup at the National Polytechnic Institute (NPI), initiated by a visiting LaoNet member and dialling a Permanet.org host in the United States. The 2002 ITU case study independently describes a LaoNet member visiting Vientiane in December 1994 and establishing dial-up email from NPI to Washington DC; it says the service was later discontinued. The two accounts support an NPI-based international email service, but do not establish a permanent local IP node or continuous operation. The exact FidoNet configuration is reported by the INET paper, not independently confirmed by the 2002 ITU summary. [ISOC INET'99](https://www.isoc.org/inet99/proceedings/3a/3a_2.htm); [ITU, *Lao PDR Internet Case Study* (March 2002), §3.1](https://www.itu.int/ITU-D/ict/cs/laos/material/LAO%20CS.pdf) (grade B).
- **December 1995 — no permanent Internet node:** Contemporary NSRC correspondence reported that there was still no Internet node in Laos. Users obtained email via the SalaThai BBS in Bangkok, commercial providers in Bangkok, or NPI’s weekly US dial-up. STENO (Science, Technology and Environment Organization, Prime Minister’s Office) and IDRC had held an Internet seminar. [NSRC, “Laos connectivity (12.95)”, 11 Dec 1995](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?id=890202381324:497429832&fromISO=LA) (grade A for the negative and contemporaneous description).
- **1996–January 1997 — PAN-Laos / STENO UUCP email service:** The 2002 ITU case study dates IDRC's PAN project for Lao email connectivity to July 1996, working with the predecessor of STEA and using a dial-up connection to a server in Singapore. The existing contemporary IDRC account gives the project and user chronology; regular full service with the UNIX base is reported from January 1997. Keep this programme separate from the 1994 NPI/LaoNet experiment unless project records establish a connection. [ITU, *Lao PDR Internet Case Study* (March 2002), §3.1](https://www.itu.int/ITU-D/ict/cs/laos/material/LAO%20CS.pdf); [IDRC PAN Laos report, Chin Saik Yoon, 16 Jan 1998](https://idl-bnc-idrc.dspacedirect.org/bitstreams/0b22b784-89c7-4ee6-92a9-7f1ed377cec8/download); [ISOC INET'99](https://www.isoc.org/inet99/proceedings/3a/3a_2.htm) (grade B).

### 1994 service-attribution conflict

A Lao P.D.R. national report included in the ITU *Telecom Development Summit: National Reports* (1999) identifies Mr Smith Xaphakdy, Chief of the Telecommunications Division, Department of Posts and Telecommunications, as the country contributor. On p. 158 it says a public e-mail centre was established by “Laonet” and IDRC, in cooperation with STENO, in December 1994; it adds that Laonet obtained a Class B IP address from APNIC, STENO was the network administrative body, and approximately 350 users subscribed. This is strong evidence for what the national report stated, but it does not identify those 350 users or say that the centre was the NPI/LaoNet experiment.

The 2002 ITU case study instead describes the December 1994 NPI dial-up as a LaoNet initiative and dates the IDRC/PAN email project with STENO's successor to July 1996. The contemporary NSRC correspondence of 11 December 1995 also mentions the NPI email setup and separately records IDRC's contact with STENO. The reports may describe two services, use “Laonet” and “LaoNet” differently, or reflect a retrospective date/attribution conflation. No original project notice, server log or APNIC allocation record has yet resolved this. Do not merge the 350-user count into LA-01 or move PAN-Laos back to 1994 without direct evidence. [ITU national reports, Lao P.D.R., pp. 157–158](https://www.itu.int/itudoc/telecom/tlc99/nat_rep.pdf); [NSRC, “Laos connectivity (12.95)”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?id=890202381324:497429832&fromISO=LA); [ITU, *Lao PDR Internet Case Study* (March 2002), §3.1](https://www.itu.int/ITU-D/ict/cs/laos/material/LAO%20CS.pdf).

### DNS and full connectivity

- **14 May 1996 — .la ccTLD delegated:** Established based on the ISO 3166-1 “la” code. Initial administrative contact: Sisomphet Nhoybouakong (employee in the Lao PDR Prime Minister’s office / STEA/STENO); technical contact: Keeper of Domains, Singapore Telecom SingNet (Singapore). Later technical changes and 2002 redelegation to Lao National Internet Committee (LANIC). DNS milestone only. [IANA Report on Redelegation of the .la Top-Level Domain, 11 Dec 2002](https://www.iana.org/reports/2002/la-report-11dec02.html); IANA WHOIS created 1996-05-14 (grade A for DNS).
- **August 1998 — first permanent international gateway:** GlobeNet established a permanent satellite link (via the Philippines) authorized in connection with a project for the state news agency Khaosan Pathet Lao (KPL). Commercial dial-up licences and services followed in 1999 (PlaNet, LaoTel etc.). [ITU case study / secondary syntheses; btw.media summary](https://btw.media/en/gateway-before-market-laos-internet-1998-1999) (grade C pending primary recovery).
- **29 January 2000 — public confirmation of full Internet:** Olivier MJ Crepin-Leblond reported the first traceroute/ping to lsc2.laonet.net (63.84.187.36), announcing “Laos with Full Internet”. [NSRC, “First Ping LA - Laos with Full Internet (2000.01.29)”](https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?id=949341656703:488940302&fromISO=LA) (grade B).

### Distinctions

Email/store-and-forward (1994–97), DNS (1996), permanent satellite/IP gateway (1998), commercial public service (1999) and confirmed public reachability (2000) are separate milestones. Local non-Internet BBS precursors (e.g., Bangkok dial-up) are recorded as adjacent, not as domestic Internet activity.

## Evidence and chronology notes

Dates for “first Internet” vary by definition (email vs full TCP/IP). The 11 December 1995 NSRC correspondence is strong contemporary evidence that no permanent Internet node existed then, while reporting NPI's weekly US dial-up email. The 1999 national report introduces a separate December 1994 public-email-centre claim (Laonet/IDRC/STENO, ~350 users) that conflicts in attribution and chronology with the 2002 ITU distinction between NPI/LaoNet (1994) and PAN/STENO (1996). Until original project records are found, keep those claims separate. The 2000 traceroute is a clear public confirmation of full connectivity. No original page captures or server logs were inspected in this pass.
