# Bhutan — early internet activity

**Status:** scouting (1994 ITU TIES dial-up/Gopher/email documented; 1999 public DrukNet TCP/IP launch with contemporary press; .bt domain transfer recorded; no resident personal homepage verified yet)

## Internet context

- **1994 institutional dial-up (ITU TIES):** Department of Telecommunications had 2400 bps dial-up access to ITU’s Telecommunication Information Exchange Service (TIES) in Geneva. Gopher was used as the browser (no World Wide Web yet) and PINE for command-line email. Used to consult ITU resources and exchange email with ITU members. International telephony tariffs applied. Retrospective account by Jichen Thinley (Bhutan Telecom), APRICOT 2015 presentation (grade C for the connection itself; contemporary character of the service is clear but exact start month not independently dated here).
- **1998 Intranet project:** Memorandum of Understanding between IDRC (Canada) and Ministry of Communications / Royal Government of Bhutan. Grant of CAD 192,888. Objective: national intranet infrastructure for email, FTP, database and document access plus international email. Implemented by Division of Telecommunications / Data Unit. Topology included Thimphu, Trashigang and Phuentsholing nodes. Contemporary project description in Thinley presentation (grade B for existence of the project).
- **.bt ccTLD:** IANA WHOIS object dated 16 July 1997. According to Thinley, when Bhutan applied, the label was already assigned to a Bhutanese tour operator and operated by a UK firm. Legal transfer to Department of Telecommunications / Ministry of Communications completed 20 May 1999. Ministry then arranged for ITU to operate the domain rather than the local BTNIC; later transferred to BTNIC/Bhutan Telecom in 2006. Grade A for the 1997 registry date; transfer details grade C (institutional recollection).
- **Public TCP/IP launch — DrukNet, 2 June 1999:** Government order in April 1999 required Department of Telecommunications to make Internet available by 2 June 1999 (three-month window). Funded by IDRC, UNDP/APDIP and Royal Government. 256 kbps satellite link to British Telecom (UK). Dial-up (initially ~33.6 kbps) available nationwide with two access numbers billed as local calls. His Majesty the Fourth Druk Gyalpo announced the service at Changlingmithang Stadium; Queen Ashi Dorji Wangmo Wangchuck inaugurated DrukNet at the Royal Banquet Hall and sent an email greeting to Bhutanese students and missions abroad. Contemporary Irish Times report (2 June 1999) and multiple secondary confirmations (grade B for the public launch and inaugural email). Also the day television was legalised. First commercial/public ISP: DrukNet (Department of Telecommunications, later Bhutan Telecom).
- **Early public access points:** First internet café opened in Thimphu around March 2000 (BBC contemporaneous report). Multi-purpose community telecentre later established in Bumthang.

## Personal web ecology / leads

- No verified resident-authored personal homepage located in current sources. Early activity was institutional (ITU TIES, intranet, DrukNet) or external (newspaper online editions aimed at overseas readers).
- Kuensel (national newspaper) had an online presence by March 1997 according to one retrospective (Tshering Cigay Dorji, 2021), primarily for readers outside Bhutan; a more widely cited online newspaper launch is given as 18 April 2001. Distinction between external hosting and local publishing should be preserved.
- Possible leads: early .bt captures, DrukNet user forums or chat services mentioned by Thinley, school or university pages after 1999, local press archives.
