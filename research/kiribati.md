**Last checked:** 2026-11-22

## Internet access context

A 2002 *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication* study, “Outside the Net: Kiribati and the Knowledge Economy,” reports that in 1998 there was no domestic Internet connection. The only way local users could access the Web was by making an international long-distance call to a server in Australia, New Zealand, or Fiji, at a reported AUD $12 per minute. Telecom Services Kiribati Ltd began Internet services at the end of 1999; the study quotes a 10 February 2001 response from a local computer-equipment entrepreneur describing a 32-modem bank, a 128 kbps backbone to Australia, and more than 400 users.

The study's March 2002 postscript says a web search found only two websites with Kiribati as their origin; one was active and published by the private entrepreneur operating Kiribati's retail computer-equipment outlet, and it also hosted the Ministry of Tourism homepage. That is a valuable early locally originated web lead, but the described page appears to be a business website, not a personal homepage. It should not be counted without evidence of individual personal expression.

## 1998 externally hosted Kiribati page (new lead)

Sofield's 2002 *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication* study reports that during the 1997/98 World Bank knowledge-economy assessment, the team showed government interviewees a “comprehensive Web page on Kiribati” that could be accessed globally. Sofield says it had been placed by a former expatriate resident of Tarawa who was living in Japan at the time. The report gives no author's name, original URL, host, exact launch date, or surviving capture.

This is a distinct lead from the locally operated computer-retail website described in the same article's March 2002 postscript. The 1998 page is an externally hosted, expatriate-authored Kiribati resource; it is not evidence of domestic Internet connectivity or of an author resident in Kiribati when publishing. Its classification as a personal homepage versus an informational/reference page is unresolved. The article's broad statement that no local government, business, or individual was using the Internet in 1998 should be read alongside this explicit external-page example, not as proof that no Kiribati-associated page existed.

## January 1997 directory-listed Kiribati Home Page (new lead)

Mark C. Goniwiecha's “Guam & Micronesia in Cyberspace,” published in the *Journal of the Pacific Society* in January 1997, includes an “Internet Resources and Home Pages” list. It names **“Kiribati Home Page, Republic of Kiribati”** and gives the original URL:

http://www.earth.tohoku.ac.jp/kiribati/kiribati.html

This is a contemporary, dated directory reference establishing that a Kiribati-labelled page at that URL was publicly listed by January 1997. The URL is under a Tohoku University subdomain, suggesting institutional hosting in Japan, but the exact 1997 server/operator and physical hosting location have not been independently established. The article's list mixes personal pages, institutions, government, commercial and community sites; the label does not identify this page's author or genre. The original URL and guessed Wayback snapshots could not be opened through this access route, and no page content or archive capture was recovered.

This may be the page described separately by Sofield in 2002 as a comprehensive Kiribati page placed by a former Tarawa resident then living in Japan (KI-03): the date and apparent Japan-based host are compatible. There is no direct evidence tying the two descriptions together, so **KI-04 and KI-03 remain separate leads**. Do not infer that the page was personal, official, written by a Tohoku employee, or created by someone resident in Japan solely from the hostname.

## 2000 Allinson briefing — MNRD as first Internet user and .ki sponsor (1995)

The 19 October 1999 (published 2000) SOPAC briefing paper by Leslie Allinson, prepared for the Ministry of Natural Resources Development (MNRD), provides contemporary institutional detail:

- In 1995, MNRD as the first Internet user in Kiribati applied for and was granted authority for the Kiribati Country Code Top Level Domain (.ki). Early management was external via Connect.com.au with the Forum Fisheries Agency as original sponsor.
- TSKL was allocated a Class C IP range (203.41.41.0–203.41.41.255) by APNIC.
- The paper documents TSKL's emerging role as Internet service provider and notes email routing arrangements (e.g., MNRD.gov.ki mail directed to TSKL).
- This places institutional email and domain sponsorship activity in 1995, well before the late-1999 commercial ISP launch, while confirming the absence of public domestic dial-up at that earlier stage.

Source: Allinson, L.J. (2000). *The role of Telecom Services Kiribati LTD as an internet services provider*. SOPAC briefing paper, 19 October 1999. https://spccfpstore1.blob.core.windows.net/digitallibrary-docs/files/66/668333c895989129571a8efc9c85e612.pdf (Pacific Data Hub dataset).

## TSKL website on the .ki domain

A contemporary directory of Kiribati resources (Wysiwyg Web Design / Mike Pearson edition of the Jonathan Willis-Richards pages), captured by the Wayback Machine on 15 July 2005, lists under “New resources”: “Telecom Services Kiribati Ltd (TSKL) – the first website on the Kiribati domain” and links directly to http://www.tskl.net.ki/. The same page notes the directory itself is hosted outside Kiribati (New Zealand / US).

Early Wayback captures of tskl.net.ki exist from December 2004 (e.g. 10 December 2004). The captured pages show an institutional telecom/ISP site with welcome text, email configuration instructions (mail.tskl.net.ki), FAQ, forum, spam management, and .ki registry links. This is operator-type commercial/institutional, medium website, activity-type service/infrastructure. It is not evidence of personal expression.

The .ki ccTLD was created on 19 April 1995 (IANA record). Early management was external (Connect.com.au / Forum Fisheries Agency); direct local management transferred to TSKL around 2002. No earlier .ki website capture or contemporary claim of a prior local site was located in this pass.

## Research interpretation

- The 1998 lack of domestic Internet connectivity is a significant constraint, not proof that no Kiribati-associated personal publishing existed. Users could connect through expensive overseas dial-up, and expatriate/diaspora pages existed.
- 1995 MNRD email and .ki sponsorship is a distinct institutional connectivity milestone preceding the 1999 commercial ISP.
- The 1999 TSKL launch and the 2001 count of more than 400 users provide a plausible period for the first locally hosted personal pages, but no individual page has been identified here.
- The entrepreneur's site named in Sofield remains an institutional/commercial lead unless a personal homepage or clearly personal section can be isolated. The TSKL site is a separate, better-documented institutional candidate for earliest known .ki website.
- Search results and later directories frequently mix locally originated pages with expatriate sites about Kiribati. Record residence, hosting, and community connection separately.
