## Internet access context

A 2002 *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication* study, “Outside the Net: Kiribati and the Knowledge Economy,” reports that in 1998 there was no domestic Internet connection. The only way local users could access the Web was by making an international long-distance call to a server in Australia, New Zealand, or Fiji, at a reported AUD $12 per minute. Telecom Services Kiribati Ltd began Internet services at the end of 1999; the study quotes a 10 February 2001 response from a local computer-equipment entrepreneur describing a 32-modem bank, a 128 kbps backbone to Australia, and more than 400 users.

The study's March 2002 postscript says a web search found only two websites with Kiribati as their origin; one was active and published by the private entrepreneur operating Kiribati's retail computer-equipment outlet, and it also hosted the Ministry of Tourism homepage. That is a valuable early locally originated web lead, but the described page appears to be a business website, not a personal homepage. It should not be counted without evidence of individual personal expression.

## TSKL website on the .ki domain

A contemporary directory of Kiribati resources (Wysiwyg Web Design / Mike Pearson edition of the Jonathan Willis-Richards pages), captured by the Wayback Machine on 15 July 2005, lists under “New resources”: “Telecom Services Kiribati Ltd (TSKL) – the first website on the Kiribati domain” and links directly to http://www.tskl.net.ki/. The same page notes the directory itself is hosted outside Kiribati (New Zealand / US).

Early Wayback captures of tskl.net.ki exist from December 2004 (e.g. 10 December 2004). The captured pages show an institutional telecom/ISP site with welcome text, email configuration instructions (mail.tskl.net.ki), FAQ, forum, spam management, and .ki registry links. This is operator-type commercial/institutional, medium website, activity-type service/infrastructure. It is not evidence of personal expression.

The .ki ccTLD was created on 19 April 1995 (IANA record). Early management was external (Connect.com.au / Forum Fisheries Agency); direct local management transferred to TSKL around 2002. No earlier .ki website capture or contemporary claim of a prior local site was located in this pass.

## Research interpretation

- The 1998 lack of domestic Internet connectivity is a significant constraint, not proof that no Kiribati-associated personal publishing existed. Users could connect through expensive overseas dial-up, and expatriate/diaspora pages existed.
- The 1999 TSKL launch and the 2001 count of more than 400 users provide a plausible period for the first locally hosted personal pages, but no individual page has been identified here.
- The entrepreneur's site named in Sofield remains an institutional/commercial lead unless a personal homepage or clearly personal section can be isolated. The TSKL site is a separate, better-documented institutional candidate for earliest known .ki website.
- Search results and later directories frequently mix locally originated pages with expatriate sites about Kiribati. Record residence, hosting, and community connection separately.
