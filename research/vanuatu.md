# Vanuatu — early Internet and Web research

**Status:** early institutional networking documented; first locally operated node identified by March 1994; first located Web service is a tourism-oriented virtual domain documented in April 1996, but hosted through an Australian provider. No Vanuatu-resident personal homepage verified yet.
**Priority:** Pacific scouting.
**Research jurisdiction:** Vanuatu (Republic of Vanuatu).

## Chronology and evidence

### 7 May 1992 — ORSTOM/RIO email access offered for the UNCED conference (contextual lead)

A contemporaneous ORSTOM/RIO announcement for the 1992 UNCED conference lists Port-Vila, Vanuatu (then labelled Vanuatu/New-Hebrides) as one of the South Pacific nodes through which participating organisations could request accounts to receive conference texts, communicate by email and join electronic forums. The announcement identifies the node as `vanuatu.orstom.fr`.

- Source: NSRC, “RIO to Rio” (message dated 7 May 1992): https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=VU&id=890202509219:497439475
- Evidence grade: **B** for the existence of the listed node and the offered conference service; the notice says accounts “should be open” on demand, so it does not prove that a Vanuatu user actually used the service or that the node was permanently operational at that date.
- Category: institutional email / conference information service; ORSTOM/RIO network.
- Qualification: this is the earliest located Vanuatu-associated network record in this pass, not proof of the country's first Internet connection.

### 1 November 1993 — RIO network context

A contemporaneous RIO status report describes RIO as an ORSTOM-developed electronic communications network supporting Internet development, with email, forums, listservs and electronic bulletins. It explains that local nodes could connect to main nodes using UUCP or SLIP over dial-up, with international communication using TCP/IP or UUCP over public data networks. The report supplies regional network context for the Vanuatu node but its general description should not be taken as proof that every RIO node had direct TCP/IP connectivity.

- Source: NSRC, “Status of RIO Network 93.11.01” (1 November 1993): https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=VU&id=890202510888:497436567
- Evidence grade: **A/B** (contemporary network status document); regional context only.

### 4 March 1994 — operational ORSTOM node in Port Vila

A contemporary technical directory identifies an ORSTOM node in Port Vila:

- Node: `vanuatu.orstom.fr`
- Link: UUCP/g/V.22bis to `noumea.orstom.fr`
- Users: 9
- Unix hosts: 1
- User access: dial-up
- Network manager: Francis Bondoux

This is the strongest early operational record found so far. It documents a local research-institution node with dial-up user access and a UUCP connection to Nouméa. It does **not** establish that the node ran TCP/IP locally or offered general public Internet access.

- Source: NSRC, “Orstom Node in Vanuatu (VU) - 94.02” (4 March 1994): https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=VU&id=890202506998:497428312
- Evidence grade: **A** (contemporary technical node listing).
- Category: research network / email and store-and-forward; operator: academic/research institution; network status: UUCP node.

### October 1994–August 1995 — Pacific Sustainable Development Network / Pactok

A message from Willie Sacksack, the Pacific Sustainable Development Network contact in Port Vila, states that the network was established in October 1994 and had about ten members by 7 August 1995. It identifies the host as “Vila Host” and gives the contact organisation as Pacific Sustainable Development Network, FSP/Vanuatu.

- Source: NSRC, “Vanuatu Networking Update (8/95)” (7 August 1995): https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=VU&id=890202506836:497430683
- Evidence grade: **A/B** (contemporary operator report).
- Category: NGO/development network; the report establishes a network and membership, but does not specify its transport protocol or prove that it was a direct TCP/IP Internet service at that point.

### 16 June 1996 — Vanuatu-labelled IP hosts and Vanuatu On Line

A contemporary host update lists the following Vanuatu-labelled names: `efate.tvl.net.vu`, `futuna.tvl.net.vu`, `malikolo.tvl.net.vu`, `santo.tvl.net.vu`, `u-e0.tvl.net.vu`, `u-s0.tvl.net.vu`, `ureparapara.tvl.net.vu`, `www.van-net.com.vu` and `www.vanuatu.net.vu`. The author explicitly cautions that some hosts were located in the United States, some had disappeared from DNS, and only a couple were reliably reachable at the time.

- Source: NSRC, “New IP Hosts VU - Vanuatu (1996.06.16)” (17 June 1996): https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=VU&id=890202506715:497432099
- Evidence grade: **A/B** (contemporary host list plus traceroute observations).
- Qualification: a `.vu` hostname or country association is not proof of physical hosting in Vanuatu. Retain the hostnames as infrastructure and archive-search leads, not as verified locally hosted services.

Dirk H.R. Spennemann's contemporaneous survey of Pacific Web servers in early April 1996 lists **Vanuatu On Line** at `www.vanuatu.net.vu` as Vanuatu's server. He reports that it actually ran through an Australian Web service, which supplied the virtual domain, and that the service was authored by an Australian company and oriented toward tourism promotion. He also criticises its stereotyped representation of ni-Vanuatu people and its limited local information.

- Source: Dirk H.R. Spennemann, “The Information Superhighway in the Pacific — Pacific Servers in April 1996” (1996): https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e28c6b09dcbd924617b2e211568884dc00add17f&repid=rep1&type=pdf
- Evidence grade: **A/B** (contemporary survey based on a field visit and a systematic server search in early April 1996).
- Category: commercial Web publishing / tourism; operator: Australian company; country connection: Vanuatu-themed virtual domain and content, but hosting and authorship outside Vanuatu.
- Interpretation: this is the earliest Web publication identified in this research pass, not evidence of a Vanuatu-resident personal homepage or locally operated national Web server.

### 24 December 1996 — Pactok still using FidoNet-style store-and-forward

A December 1996 update from the Port Vila Pactok contact, Nick Thieberger, says the network used a FidoNet system and “wantok” software developed in Sydney, with twice-daily dial-up to Sydney. The group hoped to move to TCP/IP in early 1997, but considered the local ISP's connection charges too expensive. It lists a 486 PC, Windows 3.11 and a modem, and a Pentium/Windows NT machine purchased for the planned transition.

- Source: NSRC, “Connectivity Update - Pacific S.D. Network (12/96)” (24 December 1996): https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=VU&id=890202506547:497434494
- Evidence grade: **A** (contemporary operator report).
- Category: NGO/development network / FidoNet-style store-and-forward; not yet a local TCP/IP network according to the report.
- Interpretation: do not collapse Pactok's network into the commercial Vanuatu On Line Web service or assume that the former was already directly Internet-connected.

## Assessment

The current evidence supports several distinct layers rather than one simple “Internet arrived” date:

1. **1992:** Vanuatu is named as a Port Vila ORSTOM/RIO point for conference-related email access; actual use is not demonstrated by the notice.
2. **1994:** a Port Vila ORSTOM UUCP node with nine users and dial-up access is directly documented.
3. **1994–95:** the Pacific Sustainable Development Network was operating in Port Vila with about ten members by August 1995; protocol and Internet status need further verification.
4. **April–June 1996:** Vanuatu On Line and several `.vu` hostnames are visible, but the best contemporary survey says the Web service was virtually hosted through Australia and authored by an Australian company.
5. **December 1996:** Pactok remained on FidoNet-style dial-up store-and-forward and was planning, not yet reporting, a TCP/IP transition.

No source located in this pass verifies a personal homepage authored by a Vanuatu resident. No claim is made that Vanuatu On Line was the first website associated with Vanuatu, or that no earlier local Web or non-Web service existed.

## Next research actions

1. Locate original records for the `.vu` ccTLD delegation and identify the first registrar/host arrangements; do not infer local hosting from the domain.
2. Search contemporary Telecom Vanuatu / TVL records for the first local TCP/IP service and clarify the relationship between `tvl.net.vu`, `van-net.com.vu` and `vanuatu.net.vu`.
3. Search the Vanuatu Cultural Centre / Vanuatu National Library, USP Port Vila campus and local newspaper archives for locally authored Web, email, listserv or bulletin activity.
4. Seek early captures of `www.vanuatu.net.vu` and any original `van-net.com.vu` material; preserve the distinction between virtual hosting and physical location.
5. Investigate whether the Pactok/Wantok FidoNet network was connected to Internet email gateways before the planned 1997 TCP/IP migration.
