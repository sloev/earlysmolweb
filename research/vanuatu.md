## Internet context

Vanuatu’s early documented internet-related activity consists of institutional store-and-forward systems rather than public TCP/IP or personal web publishing. Contemporary NSRC records establish two independent 1994 networks in Port Vila.

- **ORSTOM (RIO network) UUCP node (1994):** On 4 March 1994 Olivier M. J. Crepin-Leblond reported to Randy Bush an ORSTOM node at Port Vila (`vanuatu.orstom.fr`). Link: UUCP/g/V22 bis to `noumea.orstom.fr`. 9 users, 1 Unix host, dial-up access on (678) 24868. Network manager Francis Bondoux. Postal address ORSTOM – BP 76 – Port Vila. The same node appears in earlier RIO network node lists (1992–93). Evidence grade A (primary contemporary technical report). Medium: UUCP email/file transfer. Network status: inter-network (store-and-forward to New Caledonia). Operator: academic/research (French scientific agency ORSTOM).

- **Pacific Sustainable Development Network (PSDN) / PACTOK (1994–96):** NSRC provider record created 1 October 1994 for PSDN in Port Vila (principal contact Willie Sacksack). An August 1995 update stated the network was established in October 1994 with about ten members. On 24 December 1996 Nick Thieberger (`vanuamgr@pactok.peg.apc.org`) reported to Steven Huter that the group still used a FidoNet system called PACTOK with Wantok software (developed by Rob Garnsey in Sydney), connecting by dial-up twice daily to Sydney. They hoped to move to a local TCP/IP system in early 1997 but could not yet afford it. Local ISP “Vanuatu Telecom” pricing was cited as approximately US$100 joining fee, US$30/month and ~US$0.30/minute. Equipment: 486 with Windows 3.11 and modem; a Pentium 166 with Windows NT had been purchased for the planned transition. Annual subscription ~US$250. Evidence grade A (primary contemporaneous operator report). Medium: FidoNet / PACTOK email. Network status: inter-network (store-and-forward via Sydney). Operator: nonprofit/development (Pacific Sustainable Development Network / UNDP-linked project).

A June 1996 NSRC relay by Crepin-Leblond of “FI hosts” names associated with Vanuatu noted instability (some entries apparently US-hosted, names disappearing, routes looping). Host-table presence is not evidence of stable local IP service.

Secondary sources place Telecom Vanuatu Limited (TVL) commercial internet service as having commenced around 1996, but no primary contemporary launch notice, server log, or first-customer record has been inspected in this pass. No verified resident-authored personal homepage or local web publication from the 1990s has been located.
