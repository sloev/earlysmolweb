# Costa Rica — early internet-based activity

## Category chronology

### 8 November 1990 — UCR BITNET node
- Operator: University of Costa Rica (UCR), led by physicist Guy de Téramond with funding from the National Council for Scientific and Technological Research (CONICIT).
- Service: BITNET node UCRVM2, connecting San José with Homestead, Florida over a satellite link; academic email and online information-search applications.
- Status: **probable/high confidence**. A peer-reviewed historical study based on archival research and 75 interviews dates the link to 8 November 1990 and identifies it as the first BITNET link in Central America. A second scholarly account independently describes the BITNET link and the institutions subsequently connected.
- Sources:
  - Ignacio Siles, “The Internet as a transnational project: connecting Central America through computer networks (1990–1996),” *Internet Histories* (2018): https://www.academia.edu/84929106/The_Internet_as_a_transnational_project_connecting_Central_America_through_computer_networks_1990_1996
  - Ignacio Siles, “Establishing the Internet in Costa Rica: Co-optation and the Closure of Technological Controversies,” *The Information Society* (2012): https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01972243.2012.632257

### 1991–1992 — BITNET extended to other public institutions
- UCR's group connected seven other public institutions to BITNET between 1991 and 1992, installing online information-search and email applications. A 1993 report cited by Siles recorded about 1,500 BITNET users in Costa Rica across 30 organisations, mostly academic/research, plus users in El Salvador, Guatemala and Nicaragua accessing through local X.25 networks.
- Status: **probable**, secondary scholarly synthesis drawing on contemporary reports and interviews. The individual institutions and node dates should be extracted from the cited 1993 report.
- Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01972243.2012.632257

### 26 January 1993 — first direct Internet access at UCR
- Siles reports that UCR established Costa Rica's first Internet access on 26 January 1993, initially connecting 12 nodes in different UCR buildings. In April 1993, the group extended Internet links to other public universities, including the Technological Institute of Costa Rica and the National State Distance University, creating the first Internet network in Central America.
- Status: **probable/high confidence**, detailed scholarly history based on archival research and interviews; seek contemporary routing records and institutional notices for direct corroboration.
- Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01972243.2012.632257

## Evidence and interpretation
Keep the November 1990 BITNET email network separate from the January 1993 TCP/IP Internet connection. The BITNET node is a documented early academic network service, not a website. Do not infer that all 1,500 users had public Web pages.

### 26 January 1993 — institutional confirmation of Costa Rica's first Internet connection

The University of Costa Rica's University Council issued an official resolution on 2 May 2022 commemorating 26 January as the date of Costa Rica's Internet connection. Its technical chronology says the UCR established the country's first Internet connection on 26 January 1993: twelve campus nodes, a Cisco IGS router, a 64 kbps link to the National Science Foundation point of presence in Homestead, Florida. It separately dates the UCRVM2–Florida Atlantic University BITNET connection to November 1990 and notes that BITNET users reached 1,500 by 1991.

- Source: Universidad de Costa Rica, Consejo Universitario, Comunicado R-115-2022, pp. 1–3: https://rectoria.ucr.ac.cr/site/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/r-115-2022.pdf
- Status: **verified as the university's official institutional chronology**; for full technical verification, locate 1993 router/routing records and the cited 1994 project report.

This is stronger institutional confirmation of the TCP/IP date than the earlier scholarly-only note. It also firmly separates the 1990 BITNET network from the 1993 Internet connection.



## Contemporary CRNet press release — 3 February 1993

A press release distributed by the UCR Networks Unit and preserved in the NSRC Connectivity Providers Database provides contemporary confirmation of the country's first direct Internet connection. Dated **3 February 1993**, it states that UCR interconnected **10 nodes** to the Internet on **26 January 1993**, two years after establishing the first BITNET node in Central America. It describes CRNet's 64-kbps digital satellite link via RACSA/Panamsat in Costa Rica and Homestead, Florida, to the NSFNET gateway in Washington, with a Sprint fibre segment between Homestead and the NSFNET gateway.

The announcement says CRNet was intended to interconnect university, research, industrial and other national LANs. It lists prospective participants including the Universidad Nacional, UNED, Fundación Omar Dengo, SINICYT-CONICIT, EARTH, INBio, INCAE, Fundación Nahual/Huracán and the Consejo de la Tierra. It also says the Universidad Nacional de Ingeniería in Nicaragua was expected to connect in the coming weeks. This is a plan, not proof that UNI connected at that time; a later Nicaragua provider record dates the country's first direct Internet connection to February 1994.

- Source: NSRC, “Press release / Comunicado de Prensa,” last updated 3 Feb 1993; original message from `gdeter%inforisc.cr@UICVM.UIC.EDU`, dated 3 Feb 1993: https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=CR&id=890202437510:497433936.
- Evidence grade: **A/B** (contemporary press release from the UCR Networks Unit, preserved by NSRC).
- Date: 26 Jan 1993, explicitly stated in the contemporary announcement.
- Node-count discrepancy: this release says **10 nodes**. Later UCR and scholarly histories report **12 nodes**. Preserve both statements: the 1993 contemporary release is the closest dated announcement; the later records may reflect a revised count or a different counting convention. Do not silently normalize the count.

### 1993 BITNET user community — institutional breadth

A 1993 CRNet user report reproduced in Ignacio Siles's historical study records **1,734 users** on the UCR BITNET node in April 1993: 1,685 from Costa Rica, five from El Salvador, 39 from Guatemala and five from Nicaragua. Its organization table names public institutions, universities, hospitals, research centres, NGOs and businesses, including the Banco Central library, Hospital Calderón Guardia, CATIE, CONICIT, Consejo Nacional de Rectores, Universidad Nacional Autónoma de Nicaragua and Instituto Nacional Tecnológico. This documents BITNET's transnational user community, not TCP/IP Internet accounts or personal webpages.

- Source: CRNet user statistics reproduced in Ignacio Siles, *Por un sueño en.red.ado* (201iles edition), excerpt: https://es.scribd.com/document/599585706/4Por-Un-Sueno-en-red-Ado-Ignacio-Siles (tables 1.1–1.2; source credited to CRNet).
- Evidence grade: **B** (contemporary underlying CRNet statistics reproduced in a later historical study; original report still to be located).
- Qualification: distinguish 1991 estimates of 1,500 users from the April 1993 count of 1,734; do not merge them as one snapshot.


## 7 April 1995 — La Nación Digital, early national Web publication

The newspaper *La Nación* dates its first online edition to **7 April 1995**, under the name **La Nación Digital**. Its 2021 anniversary history says the paper formally began publishing on the Internet that day and identifies it as the first digital newspaper in Costa Rica and Central America. A contemporaneous second-anniversary article from 7 April 1997 confirms that the electronic edition had existed since April 1995 and describes its online archive as holding material published from 7 April 1995 onward. A 2005 anniversary retrospective identifies **Manuel Quesada**, then a newspaper IT employee, as the developer who put the first experimental version online on that date; the initial page was a simple HTML version of the printed paper.

- Sources: *La Nación*, “Dos años en línea,” 7 Apr 1997: https://www.nacion.com/archivo/dos-anos-en-linea/EP22PVANDJGXHMFWPMZ7LW5NUU/story/ ; *La Nación*, “'Nacion.com' celebra hoy diez años de labor informativa,” 7 Apr 2005: https://www.nacion.com/ciencia/nacion-com-celebra-hoy-diez-anos-de-labor-informativa/JVMIM6MJDVFXXLBGRGXXGQH4XU/story/ ; *La Nación*, “'La Nación', 75 años en papel, 26 años en digital,” 12 Oct 2021: https://www.nacion.com/el-pais/la-nacion-75-anos-en-papel-26-anos-en-digital/W3537QSMXVDRLH3EHV4MM6RYUU/story/.
- Evidence grade: **B** (publisher's own anniversary retrospectives, including a near-contemporary 1997 account; seek original 1995 page or archive capture for A-grade verification).
- Category: newspaper website / online publication; operator: commercial publisher; attributed first-version developer: Manuel Quesada.
- Qualification: this supports an early digital newspaper and its first-publication date, **not** a claim that it was Costa Rica's first website overall or the earliest personal homepage. The 1995 first experimental version and the 23 May 1995 public presentation are separate milestones in the secondary literature.
