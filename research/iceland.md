# Iceland — early personal-web research

**Status:** institutional first-web milestone documented; personal-page candidates still to be identified.
**Priority:** first wave.

## Context leads (not personal-page claims)

- The University of Iceland reports that its website went online in **November 1992**, and describes it as the first website in Iceland "as far as we know." Magnús Gíslason says he downloaded Tim Berners-Lee's WWW software from Usenet and put it online. The oldest surviving version is from 1994. This is an important institutional milestone and a promising route into early personal pages hosted by university staff and students, but the university site itself is not a personal homepage.
  - Source: [University of Iceland — The "pet project" hi.is turns 30](https://www.english.hi.is/news/pet-project-hiis-turns-30)
- The National and University Library of Iceland's web archive, Vefsafn, provides open access to Icelandic web material from 1996 onward. The archive includes Icelandic-domain material and selected Iceland-related material hosted elsewhere.
  - Source: [Icelandic Web Archive — National and University Library of Iceland](https://landsbokasafn.is/en/web-archive/)
- The Nordic library technology report notes that Icelandic schools were connected in the mid-1980s and that Ísmenn t was founded in 1992 to develop the Icelandic Network for Education and Culture. This may help locate early student/staff home directories and network lists.
  - Source: [State-of-the-art of information technologies in libraries in the Nordic countries: 1997](https://librarytechnology.org/docs/21695.pdf)

## Track A — next lead-finding actions

1. Search Vefsafn and Internet Archive captures from 1996 onward for personal homepages, not just institutional sites.
2. Search early `.is` university host directories and personal paths; inspect hi.is, hafro.is, os.is, Ísmenn t, and school network pages for named individuals.
3. Search Icelandic terms: heimasíða, heimasíður, persónuleg heimasíða, einkasíða, vefsíða, dagbók á netinu.
4. Follow link pages, guestbooks, web rings, and personal links from the earliest captured Icelandic pages.

## Track B — evidence and write-up

- November 1992 is a reported date for the University of Iceland's **institutional** website.
- 1994 is the oldest surviving version of that institutional site mentioned by the university.
- Neither date should be mislabelled as the earliest Icelandic personal website.

## Pre-Web network chronology — indirect services and 1989 IP link

ISNIC's institutional history provides a precise account of Iceland's transition from indirect network services to direct IP communication:

- Before July 1989, Iceland's international Internet connection was indirect and already carried **email and Usenet**.
- **21 July 1989 — first direct IP communication:** SURIS, an informal association of Icelandic networking practitioners (later incorporated as ISNIC Internet á Íslandi hf.), established direct IP communication between Iceland and the NORDUnet point of presence in Lyngby, Denmark. The link used IP over X.25 over satellite, with reported bandwidth of only 300–1,200 bits per second.
- The direct link added interactive **Telnet** and direct **FTP** file transfer to the existing email and Usenet services. It operated for about one year, then was replaced by a 9,600-bit/s leased line from Reykjavík to Stockholm.
- Source: ISNIC, “20 years of Internet in Iceland” (21 July 2009): https://www.isnic.is/en/news/view?id=143.
- Status: **verified as ISNIC's institutional chronology**; the underlying 1989 SURIS/NORDUnet technical records would provide independent contemporary corroboration.

### Interpretation

Do not describe 1989 as the start of all Icelandic network activity: email and Usenet preceded the direct IP link. Nor should the 1989 Telnet/FTP capabilities be mistaken for a Web site. This chronology is a useful lead for SURIS participants, university computing staff, and early personal home-directory services, while the University of Iceland's November 1992 institutional Web site remains a separate milestone.
