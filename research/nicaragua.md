# Nicaragua — early internet-based activity

## Category chronology

### 1987 — CRIES access to PeaceNet through X.25
- Operator: researchers associated with the Regional Coordinator of Economic and Social Research (CRIES), a nonprofit/NGO.
- Service: connection to PeaceNet using the regional X.25 network.
- Status: **candidate/probable** as an early online information and communications service; the 2018 scholarly history cites earlier literature for the 1987 X.25 connection. The precise service capabilities and dates of operation need contemporary corroboration.
- Source: Ignacio Siles, “The Internet as a transnational project: connecting Central America through computer networks (1990–1996),” *Internet Histories* (published online 24 Nov 2018): https://www.academia.edu/84929106/The_Internet_as_a_transnational_project_connecting_Central_America_through_computer_networks_1990_1996
- Caution: X.25 access to PeaceNet is not automatically equivalent to direct TCP/IP Internet connectivity. Keep the network/protocol explicit.

### June 1989 — Nicarao UUCP gateway to PeaceNet
- Operator: CRIES, Managua/Nicaragua; international partner: Institute for Global Communications / PeaceNet in San Francisco.
- Service: UUCP node named **Nicarao**, calling San Francisco twice daily to exchange email and provide access to news and discussion groups.
- Status: **probable**, supported by archival research and interviews reported in a peer-reviewed 2018 article. The article quotes a participant who travelled from California to help establish the node, but this is not yet a contemporaneous 1989 log or announcement.
- Territorial connection: a CRIES-operated node in Nicaragua; remote UUCP calls to a US-based service. Record this as a transnational NGO network service, not a national TCP/IP connection.
- Source: Ignacio Siles, 2018, pp. 4–5: https://www.academia.edu/84929106/The_Internet_as_a_transnational_project_connecting_Central_America_through_computer_networks_1990_1996

### 1990 — Huracán regional UUCP network (Nicaragua/Costa Rica and Central America)
- Operator: Theodore Hope, working with the Central American University Superior Council (CSUCA), headquartered in San José, Costa Rica.
- Service: UUCP-based email exchange with Internet users, connecting three times daily through UUNET; the project developed users/promoters in Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras and Panama between 1990 and 1995.
- Nicaragua connection: the founder had previously worked with CRIES in Nicaragua and developed Huracán after moving to Costa Rica in 1990. The source identifies regional activity, but does not yet identify a specific Nicaraguan Huracán node or its launch date.
- Status: **candidate** for Nicaragua-specific activity until a local node is documented; **probable** as a regional Central American service.
- Source: Ignacio Siles, 2018: https://www.academia.edu/84929106/The_Internet_as_a_transnational_project_connecting_Central_America_through_computer_networks_1990_1996

## Evidence and interpretation
The 1989 Nicarao record is a strong lead for one of the earliest documented NGO-operated internet-linked communication services in Nicaragua. It is a UUCP/PeaceNet gateway, not a website and not proof of direct national TCP/IP Internet access. The scholarly study dates Nicaragua's first direct Internet connection to 1994, which must remain a separate milestone pending technical primary-source corroboration.

### 19 March 1993 — contemporary Nicarao provider record

A contemporaneous NSRC Connectivity Providers Database entry, last updated 19 March 1993, gives a detailed operational profile for “Nicarao, the APC node in Nicaragua.” It names CRIES as sponsor, gives a Managua postal address, telephone/fax, modem number, email address (`support@nicarao.apc.org`), user fees, and X.25 access pricing for Nicaragua and Central America. This directly corroborates that Nicarao was an operating, user-facing regional network service by March 1993; it is not a Web site.

- Source: NSRC, “Nicarao, the APC node in Nicaragua”: https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=NI&id=890202429549:497430158
- Status: **verified for the provider's documented operational profile as of 19 March 1993**; the 1989 launch date and twice-daily UUCP/PeaceNet exchange remain **probable**, supported by the scholarly reconstruction pending contemporary 1989 logs.



## Contemporary milestone — first direct Internet connection, 28 February 1994

A Nicaragua country update preserved by the NSRC Connectivity Providers Database, submitted by **Josefa Yadira Mena** and last updated **3 March 1996**, states that Nicaragua marked two years since connecting to the Internet on **28 February 1994**. It says the country began with a single institution, the **Universidad Nacional de Ingeniería (UNI)**, connected by an **analogue microwave link to Costa Rica at 9.6 kbps**. This provides a precise, contemporary retrospective date and a concrete description of the initial topology.

By March 1996, the same report lists seven institutions with dedicated connections: UNI (`uni.rain.ni`), Universidad Centroamericana (`uca.rain.ni`), Universidad Americana (`uam.rain.ni`), Pan American Health Organization (`ops.org.ni`), Telematix/TELCOR, Nicarao, and IBW/Communications-Sprint. It also lists packet-radio-connected institutions and a UUCP-connected Red de Desarrollo Sostenible node preparing for a dedicated line. Named Gopher services include `gopher.uni.rain.ni`, `gopher.uca.rain.ni`, and `gopher.ops.org.ni`; WWW services include `www.ni`, `www.uam.rain.ni`, `www.ibw.com.ni` and `www.telcor.com.ni`. The report says the country had since added a 64-kbps satellite link to the United States and retained the microwave connection to Costa Rica for regional traffic.

- Source: NSRC, “Aniversario de Internet en Nic. (2/96),” original message dated 3 March 1996: https://nsrc.org/db/lookup/report.php?fromISO=NI&id=890202429101:497434871.
- Evidence grade: **A/B** (contemporary country-network coordinator report, two-year anniversary; named hostnames and topology).
- Category: first direct TCP/IP Internet access; academic gateway; later national institutional network; Gopher and Web services.
- Date precision: exact day for the anniversary claim; the report is a 1996 retrospective, not a surviving 1994 router log.

### Earlier UNI–UUNET connection: candidate lead, separate from the 1994 TCP/IP link

A 2026 review of Ignacio Siles's *A Transnational History of the Internet in Central America, 1985–2000* reports that UNI had established a UUNET link in **1989**, led by German engineer Cornelius Hopmann, before the later 1994 Internet connection via Costa Rica. This is an important lead for an earlier Nicaragua-based UUCP/Internet-gateway service, but the cited review is secondary and the original underlying source should be checked. It must not be confused with the first direct TCP/IP connection in 1994.

- Discovery source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404896992_Comentario_del_libro_A_Transnational_History_of_the_Internet_in_Central_America_1985-2000_Networks_Integration_and_Development_de_Ignacio_Siles_2020.
- Status: **lead / provisional** pending the book's exact passage, original UNI/UUNET records or a contemporary UUCP map.
- Operator attribution: Cornelius Hopmann; institution: UNI, Managua.

### Reconciliation with the 1993 CRNet announcement

The 3 February 1993 Costa Rican CRNet press release said UNI was expected to join CRNet in the coming weeks. The 1996 Nicaraguan update instead dates UNI's actual dedicated Internet connection to 28 February 1994. These statements are compatible if the 1993 announcement records an intention that was delayed; do not infer a 1993 completed link. The scholarly reconstruction also dates the 1994 connection via Costa Rica and describes the political and technical negotiations behind it.
