# Uzbekistan

**Status:** scouting

**Priority heuristic position:** 24 (after Turkmenistan)

## Internet context

Early computer communications in Uzbekistan began in the early 1990s with store-and-forward UUCP email. Users dialed Moscow or other cities via analog modems at 1200–2400 baud. Local UUCP providers operated from approximately 1992–1995 at higher speeds (9600–14400 baud). Contemporary secondary syntheses (grade C) list providers including BCC (Business Communication Center), CCC, and PERDCA (Silk.org / Silknet). FidoNet and Relcom (Russian UUCP network) were also present. Full interactive Internet access is consistently dated to 1995 in multiple sources (grade C/B for the year).

**PERDCA / Silknet (email):** The Project for Economic Reform and Development in Central Asia (PERDCA), a non-profit in Tashkent, established the Silknet electronic-mail network around 1993. A May 1995 program update (NSRC Connectivity Provider Database, last updated 5 June 1995) reports more than 1400 subscribers across Uzbekistan and neighboring republics (Kazakhstan, Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan). Subnodes were being installed so that users outside Tashkent could connect by local call. Phase Two planned Unix full dial-in access nodes. Contact: Perdca@silk.glas.apc.org / Norma@silk.glas.apc.org. This is a verified early email service (grade B, contemporary NSRC report).

**Internet / providers (1995–):** Secondary sources state the Internet first appeared in Uzbekistan in 1995, with providers including PERDCA, SOVAM, RELCOM, UZPAK, Naytov, BCC, and Silknet. By 1996 a UNDP-linked project under the Cabinet of Ministers (later associated with UzNet) and UzPAK data transmission were active. Independent international channels (satellite or Moscow modem) proliferated 1997–99. UzSciNet academic/scientific network began operations in the late 1990s. Hourly dial-up costs were around 600 soum (~US$4) in the late 1990s.

**.uz ccTLD:** Delegated 29 April 1995 by IANA (Jon Postel / USC Information Sciences Institute) to Alex Vostrikov of Tashkent as both administrative and technical contact (IANA redelegation report 10 April 2003, grade A for DNS). Registrations were free to anyone until 2001. Administrative contact later passed to Rustam Khamidov (1999) and was associated with Euracom Equipment GmbH (Berlin) / Tomas LLC (Tashkent). Redelegated December 2002 / formalized March 2003 to UZINFOCOM (Computerization and Information Technology Developing Center), which remains the registry. Wikipedia notes an A record and HTTP server for .uz since at least 1997 (grade C). DNS delegation does not by itself prove local web publishing or resident activity.

Public individual access remained limited. Government decrees in 1998–99 centralized international access through UzPAK / the national data network under the Ministry / Agency of Post and Telecommunications.

## Personal web ecology / earliest examples

No verified resident personal homepage, web diary, or local individual web publishing has been identified in the sources reviewed. Institutional, commercial, and project sites (PERDCA/Silknet, UzNet, UzPAK, later UzSciNet, government pages) appear from the mid-to-late 1990s. Diaspora or external registrations exist but are not treated as local resident activity without further evidence of connection basis. Personal expression remains a gap.

## Evidence and chronology

- Early 1990s: UUCP store-and-forward email via Moscow dial-up (secondary syntheses, grade C).
- ~1993: PERDCA establishes Silknet email network (NSRC May 1995 report, grade B).
- 1992–95: Local UUCP providers; BCC, CCC, PERDCA active (grade C).
- 29 April 1995: .uz delegated to Alex Vostrikov, Tashkent (IANA, grade A for DNS).
- 1995: First documented appearance of full Internet access (multiple secondary sources, grade C).
- May 1995: Silknet >1400 subscribers; planning full dial-in (NSRC, grade B).
- 1996: Cabinet of Ministers / UNDP Internet development project (UzNet); UzPAK begins (grade C).
- 1997–99: Independent international channels; UzSciNet; commercial dial-up expansion (grade C).
- 1999: Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 52 centralizes international access (grade B/C for policy).
- 2002–03: .uz redelegated to UZINFOCOM (IANA, grade A for DNS).
